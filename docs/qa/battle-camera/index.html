THE BATTLE CAMERA — a shot language, built where it costs nothing

2026-08-08 · battle wave 3 · ?arena=world · public/js/battle_world.js + tools/battle_camera.mjs · answers docs/plans/battle-presentation-inventory.md §10 BET B, and it is evidence for the open BET A / BET E ruling rather than a commitment to either.

BET B is gated on the diorama and free in the world. public/assets/battle/MANIFEST.md says the four backdrop plates were generated from a prompt carrying the arena camera's exact height, tilt and field of view, and that "if the arena camera moves, this paragraph moves with it and the plates are re-shot." In the world arena there is no backdrop, so the camera is free. This lane therefore built the shot language only behind the existing flag: the diorama's camera is untouched, no plate is invalidated, ?arena=world is still not the default, and flag-off is byte-for-byte today's game (§8).

What shipped. A shot table plus a solver (six kinds: round · decide · strike · impact · ko · victory), the 180-degree rule as a hard refusal inside the solve plus a 576-shot assertion, a legibility attack on the world's one measured weakness, and the two camera moves the KO lane wanted and refused — a slow push across the KO's own 760 ms hold, and a move onto the party for the cheer. It never cuts: §11.11 leaves "may the battle cut?" open for the coordinator, so every transition is an eased MOVE and cut is a per-shot policy field whose shipped value is false everywhere.

§2 THE SHOT TABLE, AND HOW EACH SHOT DERIVES

Not one metre value in the table is a camera position. The table carries INTENT — who must be in frame, how much of the frame they fill, how high the boom rides, how long the lens is, how long the move takes. The solver turns that into (aim, yaw, pitch, dist, fov) against the cast as it actually stands, using each body's own measured Box3 height and width — the same discipline the contact fix used when it derived the strike station from both bodies' widths.

Two derivations worth naming, because both were measured as defects first.

  • The subject's position is where it WILL be, not where it is. The strike shot is requested the instant act() starts, with the attacker still at her home slot five metres away. Framing the pair from there produced a wide shot of a gap she was about to close, and the frame was stale by the time she arrived. act() already derives the strike station from both bodies' widths — it now hands that position to the shot solver.
  • The boom must have a line, and so must the subject. play3d's CAMCLIP hauls the boom in when the world intervenes (it keeps running through a battle, which is how this path inherits the fix PT-20260803-025 is about) — so a shot solved into a bank is not refused, it is silently re-framed at 3 m by somebody else's easing. Each shot therefore tests the boom's own line AND nine samples across each subject's body, and lifts the boom (+0.09, +0.19 rad) rather than losing the shot.

§3 THE 180-DEGREE RULE — a refusal inside the solve, and an assertion outside it

BattleStage3D.CFG.partySide is the only place the handedness of this game is written down. Wave 3 made the world arena read it (the spike had the party's screen side hardcoded into slotsFor), and both halves of the rule read the same field:

  1. THE EYE MAY NOT CROSS THE AXIS. The axis is the line through the party centroid and the foe centroid; the side the fight was staged from is recorded at create() as axisRef, and any pose whose eye lands on the other side is refused.
  2. EVERY PARTY BODY MUST STILL PROJECT OUTBOARD OF EVERY FOE BODY. Taken with the real perspective divide, not a flat dot product — an ordering taken on a dot product is wrong the moment two bodies sit at different depths.

A refused shot is not softened into something else and shipped. The swing is halved, then dropped to zero; if the geometry still crosses (two bodies interleaved on the line) the camera keeps the pose it has and the refusal is counted on stage.cam().refusals. A camera language that can mirror the fight is worse than one angle.

§4 LEGIBILITY — the world's one real weakness, measured before and after

The BET A spike's honest verdict: "the world wins on picture and LOSES on legibility — two clean silhouettes on a low-contrast painted field is a composition; the valley is rock, foliage, houses and cast shadows." That is a claim about pixels, so it is measured in pixels.

THE METER, and it is a subtraction rather than a heuristic

Render the frame with a body, render it without: the difference IS that body's silhouette — shadow, rim, alpha and all. Render it again with the cast's depth test and depth write off and the difference is the silhouette it would have had unoccluded.

  • occlusion = 1 − (visible ∩ free) / free. An intersection, not a ratio of areas: both masks carry a halo the body did not draw (GTAO darkens the ground around a body that writes depth, bloom bleeds outward from a bright one) and the two passes do not carry the same halo — which is how a fully visible Maren first measured "−136% occluded".
  • silhouette contrast = RGB distance between the body's own edge band and the background ring just outside it, read off the background frame so a body's own rim light cannot inflate the number it is scored against. RGB, not luminance — this repo has already paid for the luminance-only version once (cutin_edge's halo term: a rim of the wrong hue at the right brightness was invisible to it on 79 of 112 shipped plates). A teal eel on tan rock is 6 units apart in luminance and 39 in RGB, and it reads.
  • foe apparent height, % of frame height, out of the silhouette's own bbox. clutter = luminance stdev of that background ring.

WHAT MOVED, PLAINLY. Foe apparent height is up at every one of the four sites, by +9% at meadow, +25% at forest, +41% at crag and +40% at water — and the worst-case foe, which is the one that actually fails to read, is up everywhere too, including 13.0% → 17.8% at crag, the site where the audit's own complaint ("the thing the player is aiming at is the smallest object on screen") was sharpest. Occlusion is equal or better everywhere. That is what the camera controls, and the lever is the shot solve: a longer lens (34°/27° against the field's 42°) with the distance solved from the subject's own box, instead of one 9.6 m boom for every fight in the valley.
AND WHAT DID NOT, WHICH IS THE HONEST HALF. Silhouette contrast falls at meadow and water and is flat at forest and slightly UP at crag, and background clutter rises at meadow and water. The cause is visible in the pictures below and it is not a bug in the solve: the fixed 9.6 m pose sometimes happens to back the cast with sky, and the shot language, framing tighter for size, backs them with mid-tone rock instead. Luminance/RGB separation from the background is terrain luck, not a camera parameter — the honest answer for it is a light or a shader on the cast, and this module deliberately writes zero shaders (that is the whole reason the r185 colour-management class of bug is deleted here rather than managed). Three.js's WebGLRenderer tests a light's layers against the camera, not against each object, so a cast-only rim light is not available without a material hook. Named, measured, and left open — see §9 residuals.

THE NUMBER WENT DOWN AND THAT IS THE HONEST RESULT, not a regression to fix by loosening the test. The spike's own board listed "two rays are not a silhouette" as a defect it had found in itself: the chest and head rays both cleared a stilt while the body behind it did not, and Maren was photographed half hidden with the solver reporting her visible. Wave 3 replaced that with nine samples across the body's own box — three heights by three lateral offsets, straddling the silhouette on the camera's own screen-right — and a slot is refused below 67% visible. The camera gained a second axis at the same time (the sweep is now yaw × pitch, because a body that no yaw can see at 0.27 rad is often in plain sight from 0.34), and that bought some of it back, but not all of it. This is the same shape as the spike's own 86.3% → 68.8% drop when its occluder set stopped being collide: the world did not get worse, the instrument stopped lying. The two changes are confounded in this one figure and separating them costs another 25-minute run that has not been made.

§9 MY VERDICT, AND WHAT IS STILL OPEN

The camera language moves me further toward the world, and it does it for a reason that is structural rather than aesthetic: in the world the camera is an instrument, and in the diorama it is a constant. Every number in §4 came out of choosing a distance and a lens per shot from the bodies in front of them. The diorama cannot do that at all — not badly, at all — because its four plates were generated from one stated height, tilt and fov, and the backdrop band is built around restPos and only restPos. Under BET E that is not a tuning job; it is a re-bake of the backdrop for every angle a shot language would want, which is exactly the "make the diorama better" answer that already failed to satisfy once.

But I would not call the world-vs-diorama question closed by this lane, and the honest reason is in §4's second box. Look at the meadow row of §1: the diorama frame is still the most readable picture on that page — two clean silhouettes, a horizon at 42%, nothing behind them competing. The shot language closes the gap that the audit named (the foes are no longer the smallest thing on screen: worst case 13.0% → 17.8%, and up at all four sites) and it does not close the gap the spike named (separation from a busy background). What it does do is change what the remaining gap costs: it is now one localised problem — the cast does not separate tonally from mid-value terrain — with known, cheap, shader-side answers, rather than a property of the whole arena.

So my recommendation is unchanged in shape and stronger in confidence: both paths, one flag, and the diorama stays as the fallback the world path already falls to. If the ruling goes to BET A, the camera language is already there and costs 0.1% of the frame. If it goes to BET E, none of this work is wasted or even touched — no plate moved, no diorama camera moved, and the flag is still off by default.

Residuals, named

  • Tonal separation from the background is unsolved and is the remaining legibility gap. The camera cannot fix it: it is the cast's own material against whatever the terrain happens to be. The two answers that would are a cast-only rim (blocked: three.js tests light layers against the camera, not the object) and a fresnel term through onBeforeCompile on the body materials only — which would give this module its first shader and re-open the r185 colour-management class it currently avoids by construction. Not attempted; it deserves its own decision.
  • Flat ring decals still clip sloped stone (a spike defect, unchanged here — they want to be projected decals).
  • The diorama still has no sides() accessor. This lane's instrument falls back to /^m\d+$/ for that column only, and says so in its own source; /^m/ matches maren, which is how the audit's impact captures once staged Vesper against her own party member.
  • The harness drives verbs against a live battle. With speed: 1 the turn loop is parked at the player's own decision prompt while the contact sheet is shot, so a frame can contain both the loop's actor and the harness's. The beats are still the beats; the actor in a given frame may not be the one named in the caption.
  • Cuts are unbuilt, not undecided-and-shipped. Every shot carries cut: false pending §11.11's coordinator ruling. The machinery to honour a cut is one branch in goTo.
