The town3 frames

Three aerials, solved from the town's own extent — the map's 45 landmarks give the centre and the radius, and the standoff comes out of the aerial lens rather than being chosen. aerial-core is the same solve at 0.55x the radius, so it is the same picture of a smaller town rather than a different composition.

The districts7 frames

One eye-level frame per map parcel — the same parcels that derive every scene contract and sceneKey, so the district set is the map's and not this lane's. The camera stands ON THE WALK NETWORK (a district frame is a place the player can be), at the standoff the target's own built extent solves for, at the lens and the min/max distances emberbrook.cameras.json already ratifies. Among the candidate stands the best nine-ray clear fraction on the target wins; a frame under 60% is REPORTED occluded, not fixed by moving the town.

What the frames ask you to rule on

A review board's job is to put the questions in front of you, not to answer them. These are the things the town-wide frames show that this lane did NOT decide, in the order they cost the most.

what the frame showswhat it iswhose call
THE HEARTLIGHT IS A WHITE CONE.district-square puts it in frame at eye level for the first time and lm_heartlight_flame renders as a hard clipped white pyramid on its plinth. This is the story's own core object -- the map's note says treat it with reverence in every shot -- and it is the one thing in the village the dressing pass deliberately did NOT touch, on the rule that a dressing layer does not re-grade the Heartlight. That rule protected it from being casually re-materialled and left it as blockout massing in the one frame the whole town is built around.coordinator's, and it wants its own small round rather than a material row: a flame is a look decision with canon attached, not a substitution.
And the same frame is the evidence the material pass works.Clay pantile roofs with real course pitch, lime plaster walls carrying their own weathering and moss, coursed rubble bases, timber posts, and the bell's post-and-lintel frame from the Festival Square hero kit -- all of it out of ONE table keyed on the blockout's own material names. 1247 slots, no object inspected, no placement moved.nobody -- this is the pass landing.
THE RIM TREES ARE BLOWN UP FROM SHRUBS, AND IT RUINS THE GAME'S FIRST GROUND.district-woodroad is the arrival clearing — the map calls it *THE GAME'S FIRST GROUND, where Vesper's arrival stages* — and it shows a trunk about 4 m across with root flares sprawling eight metres over the road, backed by a rank of ten visibly IDENTICAL specimens. The mechanism is in one line of dress_forest: a rim stand is scaled by (the blockout's proxy stand height) / (the library asset's own height), with no bound on the ratio. Where the blockout massed a 20 m stand and the picker drew a 3.2 m searsia, that is a 6.25x uniform scale — and a uniform scale takes the root flare, the bark grain and the leaf cards up with it. dress_trees has had the answer since round 1 and calls it out in its own log line: *HEIGHT TRIMMED, NOT SCALED — the library asset is already sized for its class and object-scaling would wreck its leaf cards.* The village trees get that rule. The 321 forest trees do not.mine. The fix is to make dress_forest pick an asset whose NATIVE height is near the wanted height and then bound the residual scale (the same 0.7-1.4x band the village trees already live in), rather than scaling whatever the picker drew to whatever the stand asks for. This and the lamp glass are the next round.
And the same frame shows the forest is one asset, ten times.The rank behind the Waystone is ten instances of the same scan at the same scale with only a yaw between them. The library ships two conifers and eight broadleaf-class entries; pick_for draws from the class pool on a crc, so a class with one usable member repeats. Related to the row above and not the same fault: even correctly scaled, this rank would still be one silhouette.mine, and cheaper than it looks — it is a picker-diversity question, not new assets.
The unclaimed ring: MEASURED, and it is mostly not where the far aerial says.Read off aerial-south alone this looked like the farmland ruling's unexecuted stamp — a wood standing off the houses with bare ground between. A 2 m plan census of the master says otherwise. Canopy as DISCS (crown corner over root two, not bounding boxes — 887 yawed crown AABBs read as 99% coverage of a valley that is half open, which is the same instrument error this pass refuted for walk_pad_pips-den): wood 29,860 m2 = 75% of the valley, open 7,820 m2 = 20%, water 4%, built footprint 1%. Ground that is open AND enclosed by the wood in 6 of 8 directions within 50 m — i.e. actually *between* the village and the treeline — is 416 m2, 1%, and there is no contiguous parcel over 300 m2. aerial-core shows the same thing by eye: the wood presses right in between the houses, which is the coexistence ruling holding.the open tan the far aerial shows is OUTSIDE the wood ring, on plateau the forest ruling itself says to swallow with more forest, and which no plate camera at maxDist 46 m can reach. Coordinator's, but the farmland stamp has less to execute than one frame suggested.
EVERY LAMP GLASS IN EVERY EYE-LEVEL FRAME CLIPS TO PURE WHITE.district-entrance, district-homerow and district-square all show it: the lantern's emissive glass box renders as a featureless blown white rectangle with no form, no falloff and no visible fixture. It is the same family as carried redline (a) — 680 W behind a 0.30 m glass box — but where (a) is about a SURFACE the lamp over-lights 5.9 m away, this is the FIXTURE ITSELF, in the near field, in every frame a player will stand in. Fourteen of these are the one thing this town has that no other town in the world does, and right now each one is a white rectangle.mine, and it displaces the material work at the top of my next round. --lampclamp is in the engine and defaulted off; the measurement is an emb_lum box on the glass itself, which no round has taken yet.
The implied-scale massing reads as white and orange slabs, and it is the biggest thing in two frames.far-rooftops-nw, hillside-cottages and east-cottages render as untextured white-and-orange stepped blocks with black patches, and in aerial-east they are visually larger than the village they are supposed to imply is beyond it. Two separate faults: the material pass re-surfaced 1247 slots town-wide and did not reach these, and their massing scale reads as towers rather than as a further tier of cottage roofs.the material half is mine, next round. The SCALE half is blockout-side and is the one that actually costs the frame.
The valley ground sheet has a visible edge.An aerial at 394 m sees the whole of emb_ground_valley and its cliff edge, with black beyond. No plate camera ever does — maxDist is 46 m. The aerials are a survey instrument and this is what a survey instrument shows; it is recorded so nobody reads it as a defect in the town.nobody — reported so it is not re-found.
The Heartlight reads, and it reads first.At 394 m and at eye level the Heartlight's glow is the brightest thing in the village and the lamps ring it. That is the story's own claim about this town made visible, and it came for free out of canon lighting nobody re-graded.nobody — this one is just true.

No plate camera at maxDist 46 m reaches any of it either way.the user's. Routed here rather than into a rebuild.
The Old Gate court's kit is in frame and not readable.The map's note requires the two CH1 sigil plates IN FRAME, and they are — built proud of the apron at their stamped coordinates, 87 hero-kit pieces in all. At the standoff the solver picks for gate-court they subtend about two pixels each, and the 63 flagstones read as one flat grey apron rather than as paving. The kit is correct and the frame is too far back for it.the cameras lane's, not the kit's. Recorded here because 'in frame' is not 'readable' and this town has a canon document about exactly that distinction.

The dressing lane fixed six rules this pass and did not touch the map, the cameras, the blockout's massing or the lighting grade.
