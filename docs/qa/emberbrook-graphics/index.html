Worklist item 1 of the round-3 slate in docs/qa/MORNING.md. The standing complaint is the calibrated judge's quality:water-read family on the Emberbrook checklist sweep (docs/qa/redteam/run-20260806-1/-2-emberbrook): 0 CONVINCING, 5 FAILING + 1 VISIBLE-BUT-ILLEGIBLE — the town's worst standing visual class. BEFORE images are the plates as they stood on 2026-08-02 (committed under before/); AFTER images are the live plates in /assets/scenes/emb-cine/. Serve repo /public and /docs on one port to view.

The claim

"The water surface reads as a solid black flat plane with no transparency, ripples, or soft bank contact" (pondlane) · "an untextured, flat black void lacking transparency, water flow, or reflections" (arch) · "a completely featureless, pitch-black flat plane with no reflections, transparency, or believable wet shore contacts" (gateroad) · "Water lacks transparency or surface reflection, blending into dark terrain" (therise) · "No visible water surface geometry or fluid shader is present" (homerow) · "rendered so dark and unreflective that it is indistinguishable from dark ground" (pondlane, the pond landmark).

What was measured, in the order it was measured

1 — the diagnosis holds, and it undercounted by one sheet

Measured (dry run of tools/emb_water_shader.py on emberbrook-dressed.blend): five meshes wear emb_dress_water, not the four the 09:45 census named — water_emb_pond (5,664 v), water_emb_brook (3,000 v), water_emb_millpond (656 v), water_emb_river (2,522 v after subdivision) and emb_dress_wheelpit_water (375 v), the mill's own dug pit, which the name-pattern census missed. None carried a Col layer; the material was one flat Principled at base (0.045, 0.075, 0.062), roughness 0.09, Alpha a constant 0.90, bump 0.25. Dellhollow's ratified depth→alpha recipe (docs/plans/water-transparency.md AS BUILT) had never been ported.

2 — the sheets' normals are inverted, and the ratified script would have baked the underside

Measured twice, independently — in the shipped emb-cine/scene.glb and again in the blend: on all three closed sheets the n.z > +0.5 face group sits 0.12 m BELOW the n.z < −0.5 group (pond 0.68 vs 0.80, brook 1.31 vs 1.43, millpond 4.08 vs 4.20). water_emb_river and emb_dress_wheelpit_water are open single-sided sheets with only one group at all. t2_water_shader's normal.z > 0.5 test — the ratified one — would have painted the depth fade on the underside of three sheets and skipped the river entirely, with every line it prints looking correct. The carrier finds the two near-vertical groups and takes the higher mean z as the top; a sheet with one group is open and all of it carries the fade.

3 — the obvious adaptation was wrong, and a blind pair is what proved it

Emberbrook's water is 0.10–0.39 m deep at the median (per-vertex down-ray against a local BVH). Dellhollow normalises each sheet's ramp to its own median because its bathymetry is a flat slab 4–7.5 m down; carried here unexamined, that drives four of five sheets' median pixel to alpha 0.97. So the obvious adaptation was to read the ramp in true metres — leaving the whole town's water at alpha 0.10–0.45 — with the alpha floor lifted to 0.35 so the surface kept a specular lobe, plus roughness 0.09→0.16 and bump 0.25→0.45 to break the near-mirror that reflects unlit foliage.

It lost the blind pair, 0–2. Two independent judges, given the before/after anonymised and shuffled (tools/blind_pack.mjs) with no ownership framing and no charge list, scored the change 2/10 against the untouched plate's 3/10 and preferred the untouched plate — both of them, for the same reason: "the panels carry a cooler blue-black sheen that at least gestures at wet" and the change "reads unambiguously as a paved or tiled surface, not water at all." Blender's Principled blends the WHOLE shader against a Transparent BSDF, specular included, so at alpha 0.35 the only water cue the frame had was spent on a view of unlit brown mud.
And they named the real defect, in both images: "a stair-stepped series of flat rectangular slabs, each with a visible mitred edge … like a broken staircase built from glass or ceramic risers"; "each stepped panel has a distinct trim edge where it meets the surrounding dark ground — there's no soft blend or visible bank sloping down into a water line."

4 — so what shipped is the RATIFIED recipe, plus one rule the ratified one could not have

tools/emb_water_shader.py -- save, every default as written: --scale median (the ratified normalisation), the ratified ramp 0.06 … 0.97, and the shipped surface numbers untouched (roughness 0.09, bump 0.25, base 0.045/0.075/0.062) — the blind pair's verdict, honoured. The one thing that is not Dellhollow's:

A SIDE WALL IS NOT A BOTTOM. The ratified script has two cases — top ring takes the fade, everything else goes to 0.02 — and its stated reason ("a ray entering a translucent top and leaving an equally translucent bottom renders 1−(1−a)² instead of a") is about the BOTTOM RING ONLY. Dellhollow's sheets are flat boxes seen from above whose vertical walls are never on screen. Emberbrook's brook is a stepped cascade and its risers ARE visible water faces. Three cases now: top ring → ramp(depth), bottom ring → 0.02, walls → the water's own alpha. So the mitred trim edge softens at the bank without the risers being deleted.

4b — AND THE HONEST RESULT: correctly ported, the ratified recipe is a no-op here

Measured on the shipped plate. pondlane rebaked with the recipe above, diffed against the plate it replaced: 269 pixels of 4,128,768 move by more than 8/255 — 0.007 %. Looked at side by side at 1:1, the stepped sheets are unchanged. The reason is arithmetic and was visible in the table above: normalising a 0.28 m pond to a 4 m reference is a ×14.1 ramp, which compresses the ENTIRE 0.06→0.97 fade into the first 2–7 cm of depth — a band one or two pixels wide at this camera distance. Dellhollow's version of the same recipe spans metres because Dellhollow's water is metres deep.
So this is not a shader problem, and that is the finding. Both things the judges actually named are out of a material's reach: the stacked mitred slabs are GEOMETRY — the brook is modelled as a staircase of 0.4 m boxes and no alpha ramp can turn a riser into a bank — and the black plane is LIGHTING: there is no source for the water to reflect. That is this town's own doctrine, already written in CLAUDE.md from the night-grade lane: adjusting an existing light has never moved this town; adding a new source always has. Recommended next fix class, in order: (1) reshape the brook's cascade so risers blend into bank geometry; (2) add a source the water can carry — a moon glint or a lamp sited to throw across the pond.

What the pass is still worth keeping: the diagnosed defect (zero Col layers on all five sheets) is closed, the bake is idempotent and invertible, and any later geometry or lighting fix inherits a correct per-vertex depth attribute instead of having to discover this again.

11. The one-line cause, in the judge's own source

tools/scene_redteam.mjs:477 decides whether a plate is asked about its water:

const wets = WATER_FEATURES.map((l) => ({l, cs: census(cam, l.pos)}))
  .filter((w) => w.cs.on && w.cs.state !== 'behind-camera')
It is a POINT test on the water landmark's MAP POSITION. A water body whose centre projects inside the frame arms quality:water-read whether or not one pixel of that water survives the plate. That is why arch (0.13% water), therise (0.14%) and gateroad (0.46%, none of it inside the drawn box) all carry a FAILING water verdict, and why pondlane — whose pond is entirely behind a willow — carries one whose box holds no brook. It is the same class as the Poppy defect CLAUDE.md already names: a test that projects a coordinate has not asked whether anything is visible there.
The fix is one condition, and the census that feeds it is 3 s with no Blender and no browser (section 1 of this page): arm the item only when the projected water clears a floor — about 0.5% of frame is where this town's own numbers separate the five plates that show water from the four that do not — and pass that fraction into the prompt. 15d. What was built for the sky, and the refutation it cost

THE FIRST VERSION WAS A VERTICAL RIDGE WALL AT ONE RADIUS AND IT MEASURABLY MADE THE FRAME FLATTER. A ring at 300 m is at one distance from every ray in the band, so it renders one haze value and one tone, and its only edge stood at +4.2 deg — outside the band entirely. Draft render, 1008×576, same camera and grade, top 60 rows (rows 0/30/59): L 91.3 / 64.6 / 42.0 before → 80.1 / 58.3 / 41.4 after. Darker, and no more structured. A backdrop whose whole job is depth cannot be built at a constant distance. Kept as the docstring of what replaced it.
far_horizon() — a receding skirt of land from 230 m to 1800 m (128 × 30 quads, falling −2.2 m → −211 m) with three ridge ranks standing on it, and the ranks' tops are solved, not authored: each is given an elevation band from a reference eye (pondlane's own camera z, 10.2 m) and its top is eye + R·tan(elev) —

All three edges land INSIDE the measured −6.2 .. −0.5 deg band. Haze is a CAMERA-DISTANCE mix (180 m → 0.12 air, 1500 m → 0.93), so the frame's vertical axis becomes a distance axis, which is what "mist-seated ridges" means as a shader.
emberwake_sky() — the flat constant becomes a horizon→zenith ramp (both ends DERIVED from that constant, so the hour does not move), a haze band on the horizon, stretched cloud, a Voronoi star field masked below +1.1 deg, and the moon's glow and 0.5-deg disc on the direction the plates are actually lit from. That direction is read out of emb-cine/cine.json's own appliedGrade and unanimity across every shipped camera is asserted rather than assumed — otherwise the sky and the moonlight have two authorities and nothing catches a disagreement. THE STARS, THE GLOW AND THE DISC ARE THREE ADDED SOURCES, which is the town's own measured night-grade law: adjusting an existing light has never moved this town and adding one always has.
THE LIGHT CANNOT MOVE, BY CONSTRUCTION — and that is what holds the bake set down. The backdrop is visible_camera and nothing else (visible_diffuse/glossy/transmission/volume_scatter/shadow all False, asserted per object), and the sky graph is the CAMERA half of an Is Camera Ray mix whose other half is the untouched flat Background. So every diffuse, glossy, transmission and shadow ray in the town still sees exactly the constant it saw before, and the only pixels that can change are pixels that were already at the far plane.
THE GATE FIRED, AND NAMED THE NUMBER. A backdrop nearer than the world would OCCLUDE the world on a camera ray. The inner radius is checked against the world-space bound of every non-backdrop mesh before anything is built — and on the first run it refused: the far-horizon skirt starts at 185 m and the town's own geometry reaches 206 m (emb_ground_far). 230 m is set from that measurement.
AND ONE SHARED TOOL HAD TO LEARN TWO THINGS. (1) cine_bake asserted that the world's sky-colour socket is UNLINKED whenever --sky is passed, because a linked socket would silently ignore the rig — right, and not worked around: the socket IS linked now, so the graph publishes an RGB node named RIG_SKY_COLOR and the rig is written there. One authority for the hour, and a blend that forgets the node fails loudly instead of baking the wrong evening. (2) --glb deleted every mesh that was not visible_camera and kept every mesh that was — which would have written a 1800 m backdrop into the SHARED COLLISION BUNDLE, where walkGround and the BVH would have taken it for world. If nothing but the camera may see it, it is a picture and not a place, and that is now the filter.
