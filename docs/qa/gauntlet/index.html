Overworld gauntlet loop

Five rounds of a blind-critic loop on the overworld's art. Each round: builders change something, a FRESH Opus 5 critic that has never seen this project compares our frame against real FFIX-reimagined overworld screenshots without being told which is which, and its reasons become the next round's work. The bar is artistry, not numeric similarity. Frames below are the same viewpoints every round.

Round 0 — Before — the starting point

2026-08-04, after the three.js r128 to r185 upgrade · 3/3 frames captured

Critic verdict: Ranked 3rd of 3, very high confidence. But the cause is narrow: not assets, not texture resolution, not colour taste - the lighting solve. Measured on matched ground patches, our lit-to-shadow ratio is 1.30:1 with saturation FLAT across the terminator; a golden-hour sun should be 2.5-4:1. In the critic's words: 'What A has is not a sun, it's an ambient multiplier with a slight gradient.'

What the critic asked for
  1. PIPELINE: cast and contact shadows - fourteen houses, a dozen trees, a character, a whole cliff, and not one shadow on the ground
  2. PIPELINE: a 3:1 key-to-fill ratio, with a warm key and cool ambient so shadows pick up sky rather than being the lit colour dimmed
  3. PIPELINE: recover the tonal range - the whole frame lives between 0.13 and 0.71 luminance, no true black or white
  4. CONTENT: vary the instanced dressing - rotation jitter, plus or minus 15% scale, 2-3 roof tints on the existing house asset
  5. CONTENT: the blade layer visibly stops - push density and extend the fade so the ground does not change species mid-frame
  6. DROPPED: lower the camera for a horizon - user ruling, the camera is set for the scale of world we want to explore

Round 3 — Treatment: the sun gets a direction, the distance gets air, the Heartlight gets a light

2026-08-04 — shipped as default; ?owkey= ?owenv= ?owsun= ?owbounce= ?owemit= ?owhearth= ?grade_d= ?tone=off sweep every number in it · 4/4 frames captured

Critic verdict: Not yet judged — built against rounds 1 and 2, which named the same deficit twice. LIT-TO-SHADOW, measured on the engine's OWN shadow pass (tools/ow_ratio.mjs, maskrel 0.5, four viewpoints): gate 2.40 -> 4.24, meadow 1.77 -> 3.29, vista 1.79 -> 3.92, gorge 1.58 -> 4.33, against 2.5-4:1 for golden hour. And the terminator is a HUE now, not only a level: mean (g-r) on the shadowed pixels was -2.4/-0.3/-0.2/-1.7 (flat) and is +4.0/+9.3/+9.4/+11.7 (blue-green), while the lit side goes further warm on all four.

What the critic asked for
  1. SHIPPED: the fill was the ENVIRONMENT, not the fog — a warm cos^12 sun cap supplied about half the ambient irradiance and gave the shadow side the lit side's hue. The fill is now plain sky radiance at 0.35, the cap 4.5 -> 0.30, the key x2.4, and NeutralToneMapping (hue-preserving; the 2026-08-02 ruling that rejected tone mapping was measured on ACES and AgX and still holds for both)
  2. SHIPPED: aerial perspective's third half — the far band now LOSES CHROMA toward its own luminance, graded by depth. Measured on the two daylight references, their saturation RISES toward the camera (FAR 0.30/0.35 -> NEAR 0.48/0.49) while ours was flat and roughly double
  3. SHIPPED: the fog was aimed at the sky's horizon band, but the camera tilt put four ridge rings (luma 0.24-0.60) in front of it, so far terrain faded to a value BRIGHTER than the thing behind it. Re-aimed at the ring band: the gorge's washed far slope L50 0.711 -> 0.668 and GAINS saturation 0.119 -> 0.142
  4. SHIPPED: the 'firepit' is the HEARTLIGHT, and it now casts light — one point light at the plinth, emissive ceiling 9 -> 3.4, overworld bloom radius 0.30 -> 0.15. The critic's third claim was checked and is WRONG: the 'circular gradient decal' is walk_emberbrook_green, the village green
  5. DIAGNOSED, NOT A BUG: the gorge's 'straight-line terminator into overexposure' is the plateau CREST — real terrain — with the slope beyond it washed by fog. Fog off moves that patch L50 0.711 -> 0.593, the tone curve moves it 0.738 -> 0.711; it was fog, and it is now largely gone
  6. STILL OPEN: the two grey rectangles over the gorge sky. Untouched this round
  7. STILL OPEN (CONTENT lane): the near-field blade carpet reads as oversized at the gate and meadow cameras, and the frame's near-band saturation is 0.70-0.79 against the references' 0.48-0.49

Round 4 — The grade goes into the light; the artefacts get named

2026-08-04 04:30 · 4/4 frames captured

Critic verdict: Ranked 4th of 4. The critic's headline: 'There is not one cast shadow in the entire frame… the houses have no lit side and no shade side. Four walls, one colour.' And its summary of two frames from the same build: 'A got a colour grade and B got a light.' It ALSO caught an over-correction I had missed after looking myself: the golden grade is past the point where hue distinctions survive.

What the critic asked for
  1. FIXED: the grade was multiplying EVERY near pixel by a warm colour - a global tint arriving through the post pass, the exact defect the lighting round existed to remove. Now weighted by each pixel's own luminance: full on lit surfaces, nothing in shadow.
  2. FIXED: the key's blue channel was closed. (1.0,0.70,0.42) at 2.4x turned grass albedo into yellow - NO lit surface could read green whatever its albedo.
  3. FIXED: HALF THE BLADE CARPET WAS RENDERING BLACK - three.js's DOUBLE_SIDED normal flip negating the blade's authored up-normal on back faces, with yaw uniform over 2pi, so ~half of 177k instances pointed at the dirt.
  4. FIXED: the grey rectangles were THE AO PASS'S OWN DEPTH BUFFER. GTAOPass draws its prepass with scene.overrideMaterial = MeshNormalMaterial, which carries neither the leaf atlas nor its alphaTest - so every alpha-cut foliage card writes its WHOLE QUAD into the g-buffer, and a camera-facing billboard is axis-aligned in screen space by construction. The aerial grade then read 11 m where the picture shows a 233 m ridge.
  5. FIXED: the 'unlit white trees' and the 'missing skybox' were ONE NUMBER - the fog was aimed at a colour between the two FURTHEST ridge rings while the terrain's skyline is drawn against ring 0.
  6. REFUSED: going all the way to the refs' near-band saturation was tried and the frame got worse - milky, flat trees, amber graded away. Stopped at 0.60-0.70 and said so.

Round 5 — The houses were too small to cast a shadow the sun could draw

2026-08-04 05:40 · 4/4 frames captured

Critic verdict: THE ROUND THAT PAID FOR THE LOOP. Four rounds of critique named 'no directional light'; chasing it directly produced one regression and one cleanup. The cause was never the light. THREE blind critics said 'the character is roughly one house tall' from looking, and a shadow lane derived the same thing from geometry: cottages 2.6 m tall and 2.9 m WIDE, height:width below 1, so at 34 degrees the shadow barely outran the footprint that threw it. The only object in the frame with a visible shadow was the character - the only thing taller than it was wide.

What the critic asked for
  1. ridge height 1.60u -> 3.70u; 1.10x the character -> 2.65x (2.97x to the chimney cap)
  2. height:width 1.10 -> 1.39; shadow at 34 degrees 2.4u from a 1.5u footprint -> 5.7u from a 2.76u footprint
  3. Gained eave board, steep gable, gable-end chimney, person-sized door, stone footing that reaches BELOW the lowest footprint corner so a house on a slope is not cut by a straight line
  4. BROKE AND FIXED: a house landed ON the 'Enter Emberbrook' portal - the old road-clearance push was RADIAL, which for a house bearing along the road walks it DOWN the road. Every gate was green with it there; only the frame showed it.
  5. BROKE AND FIXED: Dellhollow lost a third of its stations (11 -> 7) to wider terrace pads. Back to 11.
  6. OPEN, needs the coordinator: the Heartlight is now a garden ornament among 4u houses. Geometry and play3d's point light are both hard-coded at village_h+1.34 and must move in one commit.
  7. NOT TRIED - RAN OUT OF NIGHT: ground de-tiling and the water shoreline. Both are terrain-material work sitting one step from the grade.
