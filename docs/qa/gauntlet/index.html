Overworld gauntlet loop

Five rounds of a blind-critic loop on the overworld's art. Each round: builders change something, a FRESH Opus 5 critic that has never seen this project compares our frame against real FFIX-reimagined overworld screenshots without being told which is which, and its reasons become the next round's work. The bar is artistry, not numeric similarity. Frames below are the same viewpoints every round.

Round 0 — Before — the starting point

2026-08-04, after the three.js r128 to r185 upgrade · 3/3 frames captured

Critic verdict: Ranked 3rd of 3, very high confidence. But the cause is narrow: not assets, not texture resolution, not colour taste - the lighting solve. Measured on matched ground patches, our lit-to-shadow ratio is 1.30:1 with saturation FLAT across the terminator; a golden-hour sun should be 2.5-4:1. In the critic's words: 'What A has is not a sun, it's an ambient multiplier with a slight gradient.'

What the critic asked for
  1. PIPELINE: cast and contact shadows - fourteen houses, a dozen trees, a character, a whole cliff, and not one shadow on the ground
  2. PIPELINE: a 3:1 key-to-fill ratio, with a warm key and cool ambient so shadows pick up sky rather than being the lit colour dimmed
  3. PIPELINE: recover the tonal range - the whole frame lives between 0.13 and 0.71 luminance, no true black or white
  4. CONTENT: vary the instanced dressing - rotation jitter, plus or minus 15% scale, 2-3 roof tints on the existing house asset
  5. CONTENT: the blade layer visibly stops - push density and extend the fade so the ground does not change species mid-frame
  6. DROPPED: lower the camera for a horizon - user ruling, the camera is set for the scale of world we want to explore

Round 2 — Camera: an aim tilt puts a horizon in frame for the first time

2026-08-04 — shipped as default, ?owtilt=0 restores the old frame · 4/4 frames captured

Critic verdict: Ranked 3rd and 4th of four (meadow above gorge). The gap is large and it is NOT mostly asset budget: the top four fixes are all TREATMENT. The critic's summary — 'light is currently a GLOBAL TINT rather than a DIRECTION: every surface got warmer, but nothing got occluded, so nothing has form. Fix that one thing before commissioning a single new asset.'

What the critic asked for
  1. TREATMENT: cast shadows with one committed key direction — nothing casts one; largest single change available
  2. ASSET: a near-field ground-detail tier, dense at the bottom of frame and thinning with distance — the current tuft is too sparse, too uniform, too spindly to read as ground cover
  3. TREATMENT: the firepit is CLIPPED to pure white over ~60px with a bloom ~3x its plausible radius, over a circular gradient decal that does not follow the terrain and does not light the houses beside it
  4. TREATMENT: depth fog / aerial perspective — the tan hill upper-right is as saturated and contrasty as grass three metres from camera
  5. BROKEN (gorge): two hard-edged grey rectangles over the sky top-left — 'reads as a crash artefact'. INDEPENDENTLY CORROBORATES the camera lane's own unfixed 'pale grey rectangle upper-left'
  6. BROKEN (gorge): plateau grass upper-right blows to pale blue-white with a STRAIGHT-LINE terminator — a slope does not do that; fog volume or bloom threshold
  7. BROKEN (gorge): several ridge trees render pure white/unlit while their neighbours are green
  8. TREATMENT: anti-aliasing — roof ridgelines and canopy edges are stair-stepped at 1x
  9. ASSET: water shader and shoreline — flat opaque planes butting terrain on a clean edge, no shallows, no wet band
