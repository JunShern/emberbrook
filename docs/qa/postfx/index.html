The post-processing stack

2026-08-03. Same viewpoints, same scenes, same harness as the r185 board (tools/three_shots.mjs against docs/qa/three-upgrade/shots.json), so this strip chains to that one. BEFORE is HEAD before this lane; AFTER is HEAD after it. Numbers from tools/shot_compare.mjs. Toggle everything with ?postfx=off.

The stack is per scene family, and that is a measurement, not a preference.
  • Real-time scenes (ow-*, emb-townwalk) get the whole thing: RenderPass → GTAO → aerial split → bloom → OutputPass, on 4× multisampled half-float buffers.
  • Baked-plate towns (emb-*, del-*) get nothing. The composer is never built and those five frames are bit-for-bit what they were. Two reasons, either sufficient — see what we rejected.
  • The battle arena has its own renderer and its own four-pass grade; the one change there is that its scene buffer now asks for MSAA, which it had silently been doing without.

The one that carries the lane — ambient occlusion

Nothing in this game has ever had any. The real-time scenes are honest geometry under a solved key with no baked lighting, so a pavilion post, a cottage sill and a box all met the ground on a hard albedo seam. Look at the character's feet, the crate on the left, the paving slabs on the right, and the cobbles in the plaza — which were not visible at all before.

L05 0.175 → 0.166 · L50 0.424 → 0.415 · chroma 0.149 → 0.153. She is standing on the ground rather than in front of it. The frame gets slightly darker because contact shadow is, correctly, darkness that was not there.

L05 0.171 → 0.144 · L50 0.431 → 0.412 · L95 0.586 → 0.518 · chroma 0.128 → 0.132. The biggest single change in the set: the cobbled plaza on the left is geometry that was already there and could not be seen. The pavilion posts, the lamp foot and the crates all acquire footprints.

The overworld

L05 0.237 → 0.238 · L50 0.473 → 0.484 · chroma 0.221 → 0.218. Cottage windows and the square's fire now read as light sources, and the near clearing separates warm from the cool far meadow. Bloom threshold is 0.99 on purpose — see the rejected frame below.

L05 0.168 → 0.179 · L50 0.421 → 0.427 · chroma 0.096 → 0.125 (+30%). The largest chroma gain in the set, and it is the one the overworld-art lane asked for.

The aerial split — a depth-graded hue rotation that holds saturation

Our four ow-* cameras are fixed high and looking down, so a shipped frame has no horizon and no sky to tint: the usual "cool the far background" move has nothing to act on. The content lane measured that naive distance fog is the wrong tool — it goes milky long before it goes blue, and dropped far-band saturation below the references' own floor. This pass does the other thing instead: a multiplicative warm/cool tint by radial distance (view position reconstructed from GTAO's own depth buffer, so the cue radiates from the character rather than running up the screen the way Fog's -mvPosition.z does). Hue separates; luminance and local contrast stay where the art direction put them. The shipped fog (34/205) is untouched.

Near-patch minus far-patch red-blue difference (the warm-near / cool-far split): 12.9 → 29.5 on ow-corridor, 19.5 → 26.3 on ow-corridor-turn. Knobs: ?grade=0..1 amount, ?grade_k tint strength, ?grade_n/?grade_f the distance ramp.

The baked-plate towns — deliberately identical

L05 0.014 → 0.014 · L50 0.153 → 0.153 · L95 0.498 → 0.498 · chroma 0.090 → 0.090. All five plate frames are identical to three decimals. That is the deliverable, not a miss.

What we rejected, with the frames

1 — the composer on a baked-plate town

three r185 turns material-level tone mapping off whenever the destination is a render target (WebGLPrograms.js), because the composer's OutputPass is meant to apply the curve at the end. But the plate does not arrive through a material — it is scene.background, and this page deliberately strips #include <tonemapping_fragment> out of ShaderLib.background so a plate baked through Blender's AgX is not graded twice. Under a composer that exemption is lost.

L05 0.014 → 0.000 (the toe crushes to true black), L50 0.153 → 0.133. By eye it is worse than the numbers: the night reads as flat overcast dusk, the lamp pools lose their warmth and their edges, and the foliage lifts. Screen-space AO on that frame would also be computed against depth.png's reconstruction of a picture that already carries Cycles' own AO. Reproduce with ?postfx=all.

2 — bloom at a threshold that catches the floor

These scenes render with NoToneMapping, so nothing in them is HDR: the brightest ground is already near 1.0 linear and the only things above it are emissive window panes, lamp heads and the square's fire.

The aggregate barely moves (L05 0.144 → 0.157, L50 0.412 → 0.413) and that is the point: the percentile table cannot see this, and the picture can. The plaza hazes, the cobbles glow, the tree speckles. A difference table is not the instrument for a veiling glare.

The battle arena — MSAA it had been doing without

new WebGLRenderer({antialias:true}) asks the browser for a multisampled default framebuffer. The moment the arena's four-pass grade landed, the scene started rendering into post.rt instead, and that request bought nothing: every silhouette in the arena has been hard-aliased ever since, with the renderer configured for antialiasing. One line — samples: 4 on the scene buffer.

Mean horizontal edge energy 5.725 → 5.591 (−2.3%). Honest caveat: the arena scatters its reeds and rocks per boot, so the two frames are not the same picture and the number is confounded by prop count. The mechanism is not in doubt — the render target was allocated with samples unset — but this instrument cannot isolate it.

The lighting probe — what the blind critic's 1.30:1 actually is

Ratio measured with a shadow mask taken from the engine's own shadow pass (the pixels that darken when dl.castShadow is toggled) rather than from matched patches, so "shadowed" is not a guess.

  • The shadows exist. The shadow pass moves 20.2% of the sampled ground and props by more than 12/255. "Not one shadow on the ground" is wrong as stated — they are real, they are just only 1.36:1 deep, so they do not read as shadow.
  • It is not penumbra averaging. Banded by occlusion strength, the deepest core (>60/255 removed) is 1.34:1 and the penumbra is 1.30:1. The ratio does not deepen with occlusion.
  • The environment is the fill. scene.environmentIntensity = 0 takes shadowed ground from 90.2 → 55.8 and lit from 122.9 → 103.7 — ratio 1.36 → 1.86:1. The key contributes only 43/255 of a lit pixel; the bounce contributes 7; the fog contributes nothing measurable (90.2 → 90.0 with ?owsky=0), so the floor is not being lifted by fog.
  • An instrument defect worth recording: window.__envTune(k, sunLevel), the documented live surface for this fill, does not move it. Swept at k = 0.75 / 0.45 / 0.20 the shadowed ground sat at 90 / 90 / 91. scene.environmentIntensity is the knob that works.
  • Key alone cannot get there. Key ×1.8 / ×2.6 / ×3.4 takes the ratio 1.36 → 1.56 → 1.73 → 1.86 while L99 goes 0.727 → 0.830 → 0.902 → 0.957. The highlight end clips before the ratio arrives, so golden hour here needs the fill cut and the key raised together.
