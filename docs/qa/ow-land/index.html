ow‑valley — the landscape

The forest probe missed, and this page starts by saying why. F1/F2/F3 all worked at the level of “make the trees more varied”. The reference frames contain no forest at all — so what they are pointing at was never a tree‑species problem. It is whole‑landscape: light, air, ground detail and blended transitions. Everything below is diagnosed and judged at that level.

These are AAA renders from a professional studio and this is a prototype. Parity is not on offer and nothing here claims it. What is on offer is the two or three techniques that close the most perceived distance for the least work, each measured, each with the gap it does not close stated in the same breath. Nothing on this page ships: the bundle, the map files and play3d.html are untouched; every candidate is injected into the live runtime over CDP and photographed.

The bar, in words, before any of ours

REFERENCE 1 — a cliff‑top meadow with a worn path, a cave mouth in a pale outcrop, sea and headlands behind. The ground is grass GEOMETRY: blades break the outline at the ridge, at the rock's foot and along the path, and a few clumps stand on the path itself. Nowhere is there a hard edge — the worn earth is encroached on by grass, loose stones bridge grass to rock, plants grow out of the rock's cracks. Vegetation is clustered at three scales (dark foreground bushes bottom‑left and bottom‑right, mid shrubs around the outcrop, a green haze on the far headland) with white / yellow / pink flowers in patches of five to ten, never evenly spread. The rock has vertical fracture and horizontal bedding and a real range of tone. And the light is doing most of the work: lit grass is a bright yellow‑green, shadowed grass is a deep BLUE‑green — a hue shift, not just a darker version of the same colour — and the character sits in a clean pool of contact shadow.

REFERENCE 2 — night, so almost all of it is one dark blue‑green field. The whole picture is carried by one hue contrast: warm orange windows against a wholly cool landscape, with a clean soft cast shadow under the character. Grass still silhouettes. Bushes cluster at the cliff base, along the left edge and at the building's foot — there is no bare junction anywhere between a built thing and the ground.

REFERENCE 3 — the one that is almost entirely ATMOSPHERE. Near meadow saturated yellow‑green, mid valley pale sage, far mountains near‑white blue, with fog physically banked in the valley. Depth is carried by haze, not by size. A big luminous sky is a third of the frame, a dark rock and tall grass frame the bottom, and daisies sit in patches. Nothing in it is a better asset than ours — it is better staged and better lit.

The through‑line across all three (and across the two town frames and the Dagger portrait too): one strong key, a real value range top to bottom, and a WARM‑LIGHT / COOL‑SHADOW split. That is the thing they all share and it is the thing we do not have.

THREE FINDINGS, AND NOT ONE OF THEM IS ABOUT AN ASSET.

1. THERE IS NO LIGHT IN THE CORRIDOR.  Three of our four DAYLIGHT cameras are
   darker than the reference's NIGHT plate (L50 0.204 / 0.236 / 0.243 against
   Treno's 0.245).  And NOTHING anywhere in any of our frames is brighter than
   0.536, where both daylight references put 5 % of the frame above 0.70.  We
   have no highlight at all — no lit ground, no sky, no bright rock.  The frame
   has a floor and a ceiling and they are 0.07 apart from each other.

2. NOTHING IN THE CORRIDOR IS COOL.  b−r is NEGATIVE in both the dark and the
   light quartile of every one of our four frames.  Reference 1's shadows measure
   +0.090 — actually blue.  Ours are −0.06 to −0.10: brown, only darker.  A
   shadow that is the same hue as its lit surface is a multiply layer, and that
   reads as a rendering trick rather than as a place.  It is also why 40-59 % of
   our coloured pixels sit in ONE 30-degree hue bin against the references' 32-43.

3. THE GROUND HAS A THIRD OF THE REFERENCE'S PIXEL-SCALE DETAIL.  detail 19.3 at
   walking closeness against 29.7-48.1.  That is the "flat tinted plane" — the
   thing asked about twice — as a number.

WHAT IS *NOT* WRONG, so it does not get chased again: the sun angle (ratified, and
the towns' own key transformed), the shadow pass (working — see §4 below for the

(150,528 for 280 x 200 u; the corridor is not under-modelled).

Before — the four cameras

The first probe's own four: the Emberbrook gate at boom 16, the gorge shelf at walking closeness (boom 12), the shelf at the shipped follow camera (boom 40) and the Dellhollow approach (boom 40). These are the “before” in every row below, re‑rendered at this page's frame size so every number on the page is comparable to every other.

What I see. At the gate, a tan plateau meets a green meadow along a jagged vector polygon and the road crosses both as a flat brown ribbon with a cut edge on each side; nothing on the ground is taller than the paint. On the shelf at boom 12 the grass is a low‑frequency olive smear with faint streaks in it, and the whole frame — cliff, road, grass — sits inside one narrow brown band. At boom 40 the picture is a brown wall, an olive ribbon and a cyan river, and the far rock is exactly as saturated as the near rock, so the gorge has no depth. On the Dellhollow approach the meadow at left is a single unbroken tan‑olive gradient the size of a third of the frame. In all four: no sky, no haze, no highlight, and every boundary is a cut.

L1 — THE HOUR +0 triangles

Light, air, and the shadow side. The key is NOT touched — its angle and colour are the towns' ratified golden hour and this lane does not get to re‑decide them. What changes is everything the key does not reach. The fill is raised and made properly blue, and raised on the HEMISPHERE rather than the ambient, because a hemisphere is directional and keeps the top‑to‑bottom gradient that gives a solid its form — flat ambient is exactly the “1.70 of omnidirectional fill” mistake the light lane already paid for. One dim cool shadowless bounce comes in from roughly opposite the sun, the second thing every reference has that we do not: a shadowed vertical face that is not flat. And the fog is brought in from 150 m to 34 m — the shipped fog starts at 150 m, and the walked corridor is 172 m end to end, so nothing the player ever looks at inside the valley is far enough away to be touched by it. It reaches only the vista ring beyond the map, which the play camera never sees (§5).

What I see. The shadowed half of every frame stops being a hole. On the shelf the ground inside the cliff's shadow goes from a dead brown‑black to a readable cool blue‑green, and you can now tell that it is grass in shadow rather than an unlit patch; the cliff's own shadowed face picks up a gradient instead of one value. At boom 40 the far end of the gorge takes a faint haze and the frame gains depth it did not have. The whole picture is also flatter in contrast and less saturated — that is the honest cost of raising fill, and it is why L1 wants L3 next to it.

Measured. L05 (the darkest twentieth of the frame — i.e. the shadows) 0.094→0.150 at the gate, 0.108→0.154 at boom 12, 0.072→0.146 on the Dellhollow approach: a doubling of the value in the darkest part of every frame. L50 up 15–30 %. Saturation down (0.59→0.44) and detail essentially unchanged — light cannot invent texture.

What this deliberately does NOT do. It adds no filmic tone curve. That was already tried and looked at (docs/qa/charlight/tone-ow-{off,aces,agx}.png) and the finding stands: at exposure 1.0 both AgX and ACES take the meadow to khaki and the river to grey‑teal, because this palette is direct albedo under a key solved in linear. The fault in these frames was never the curve. It is the fill.

L2 — THE GROUND IS GEOMETRY +113,924 triangles

Detail placed only where it makes an edge. The reference's grass reads as grass because blades break the SILHOUETTE — against sky at a ridge, against rock at a cliff foot, against worn earth at a path edge. It is not a carpet, and a carpet is what the first probe's A1 was and what was refused. So the placement rule IS the candidate: a tuft is emitted only where the ground CHANGES, at a density falling off over ~2.6 m, and nowhere else. That answers three complaints in one pass — no scale cue on the ground, hard vector transitions, and vegetation that is not clustered at multiple scales. Three scales are built: 17,837 six‑triangle blade tufts at the seams, 261 shrub clumps at rock feet, and 841 flowers in 89 patches (a patch of six to fourteen, never an even scatter — that difference is the whole trick and it costs nothing to obey).

What I see. At boom 12 the road stops being a decal: its edge is now a ragged fringe of grass leaning over worn earth, with a scatter of white flower heads in the verge beside the character, and the ground has a scale you can read the body against. At the gate the tan/green polygon boundary is broken by tufts standing on both sides of it, so the plateau reads as dry ground the meadow is thinning into rather than as a paint splotch. On the Dellhollow approach the shelf reads as a grassy ledge above the gorge.

Measured. detail 19.3→33.2 at boom 12 (+72 %, which takes us past reference 3's 29.7), 25.3→33.4 on the Dellhollow approach, 34.1→40.6 at boom 40. L50 and the hue numbers barely move — this candidate is texture and nothing else, which is exactly why it composes with the other two rather than competing with them.

What it costs and what it is honestly worth. 113,924 triangles is real spending and it is the only candidate here that spends any. Two things about that number. First, the shipped bundle already carries 38,740 triangles of meadow scatter and 9,792 of tree trunks that land ZERO triangles within 15 m of the road at all seven sampled points (first probe's census) — a third of this is available by moving budget rather than adding it. Second, a tuft is six triangles; the cost is the count, and the count is a knob. At half density the frames still read and the bill halves.

The shapes are stand‑ins and the clumps are the weakest of them — a squashed icosahedron with a borrowed bush map still reads as a faceted green gem at close range. Judge the density, the scale against the 1.45 u body and the placement rule; not the modelling.

L3 — THE SURFACE +0 triangles

“Improve what exists” taken literally: the terrain we already have is the wrong colour and has no variation. Nothing is added and nothing is moved; only COLOR_0 changes, on the three ground batches. The grass is lifted and greened, because it is currently darker than the rock. The rock is COOLED and given BEDDING — a banding in world y, which is what strata is — plus a slow tonal drift so that no two faces of one wall share a value. And every vertex near a surface change is dragged toward the other surface's tone through a noisy threshold, so a seam becomes ragged instead of vector.

What I see. The gorge wall stops being the warmest thing in the picture. It goes to a cool grey‑brown with visible horizontal banding and reads as bedded sandstone rather than as one poured slab, and because it has cooled, the green shelf in front of it now separates from it instead of blending into it. The meadow at the gate becomes a real green with patchy variation across it rather than one olive wash, and the tan plateau warms against it, so the two zones read as two materials even before any tuft is planted.

Measured. L50 0.363→0.428 at the gate and 0.243→0.263 on the Dellhollow approach, with Lrange up in both (0.442→0.517 and 0.357→0.404) — it adds contrast rather than spending it. Shadow warmth b−r dark −0.058→−0.031 at boom 40. One‑hue mass at the gate 0.588→0.514. detail is flat, as it must be — there is no new geometry.

What this cost to learn. The first pass multiplied the rock's blue channel by 1.10 and the cliff did not move at all. The brown is in the MAP, and COLOR_0 multiplies it, so a 10 % push against a channel that is already the smallest is nothing. A saturating control — forcing each ground batch to pure red / green / blue — proved the plumbing in one frame and located the fault in the amplitude, and the working number turned out to be r x0.66 / b x1.62. A null result on a recolour has to be separated from a broken recolour by an amplitude that cannot be missed; the forest lane paid for the same lesson last night and it is worth paying only once.

§4 — A BUG, not a candidate: the road never receives a shadow

Found while measuring, and it is one regex. play3d.html's overworld rig walks the bundle with if(o.isMesh && !/^(walk|bar_|__ow)/i.test(o.name)) and gives everything else castShadow and receiveShadow. That filter exists to keep the invisible walk‑network meshes out of the shadow pass — but in ow‑valley walk_road IS the visible road ribbon, and there are five such visible walk_ meshes. So the one surface the player is always standing on is excluded from the light. Both frames below are the shipped bundle under the shipped rig; the only difference is the flag.

What I see. In the shipped frame the road runs as a bright ochre stripe straight through the deep shadow that the gorge wall throws across the grass on both sides of it — the shadow stops at the path edge and resumes on the far side. With the flag on, the shadow's edge runs continuously across grass, road and grass, and at boom 12 the character now stands at a light/shade boundary on the path she is walking on.

Measured. 15.0 % of the boom‑12 frame and 5.0 % of the boom‑40 frame move by more than 8/255, and the pixels that move go from L 0.238 to L 0.099 — they are the road entering the shadow it was always standing in.

This is not mine to change: play3d.html is coordinator‑owned. Flagging it with the A/B and the number. The fix wants to be narrower than the regex — something like “a walk_ mesh that is VISIBLE in a real‑time region receives (and casts)” — and it should be checked against the townwalk scenes, which use the same filter.

§5 — And one thing no candidate can fix: the camera never sees the sky

Every reference frame gives 10–30 % of its picture to sky and haze, and reference 3 is carried almost entirely by it. All four of our cameras contain zero sky pixels. The follow camera sits at pitch 0.61 / boom 40, which points it into the ground; the gradient dome, the three rings of receding ridge silhouettes and the horizon-matched fog that were built on 2026-08-02 are, in gameplay, invisible. Below: the same spot at the shipped pitch, at pitch 0.26, and at pitch 0.26 with L1+L3.

What I see. At pitch 0.26 a pale sky and a distant ridge enter the top fifth of the frame and the picture acquires a horizon to measure everything else against; with L1's near fog the ridge goes hazy blue and the valley finally has the near‑mid‑far sandwich that reference 3 is built out of. I am NOT proposing a camera change — pitch is a gameplay decision about how much ground the player can read ahead, it belongs to whoever owns the follow camera, and a lower pitch costs legibility of the path. It is reported because it is the largest single difference between our composition and theirs, and because it means work already paid for is currently not visible to anyone.

What I would do — and this is mine to reject

L3 first, then L1, then L2 at whatever density the frame rate allows. And §4 regardless of all of it.

1. §4 is not a candidate, it is a defect, and it is one line. 15 % of a walked frame is wrong today for free. It should go in whether or not anything else on this page is picked.
2. L3 is the best value on the page. Zero triangles, no new material, no rebuild of anything — a recipe in tools/valley_build.py where the terrain COLOR_0 is already written — and it moves L50, Lrange, shadow hue and one‑hue mass all at once. It is also the one that fixes the finding I would most defend: the grass being darker than the rock is why the corridor reads brown, and no amount of light or grass geometry corrects an inverted albedo.
3. L1 next, because it owns the shadows and nothing else does. L05 doubles in all four frames. Its cost is that it flattens contrast and desaturates, which is exactly the hole L3 fills — that is why I would take them as a pair rather than choose between them. It is a change to play3d.html's ow rig, so it is a coordinator edit, and it is worth saying plainly that this re-opens a lane that was declared closed. I re-opened it because all six reference images are, first and foremost, pictures of light.
4. L2 last, and it is the only one I would stage. It is the only candidate that adds volume, and adding volume is the thing that was refused — I am putting it forward anyway because the specific complaint (“the ground is a painted plane”, asked twice) cannot be answered without geometry, and because detail is the one measured column that light and colour cannot move. But it is a knob: half the density still reads, and the first tranche should be the road verge alone, which is the ground the player actually looks at.

WHAT REMAINS OUT OF REACH, stated plainly.
  • The highlight. Our best frame reaches L95 0.675 against reference 1's 0.799, and our sunlit meadow p90 0.607 against its 0.806. Closing that needs a tone curve that lifts without desaturating — a graded‑renderer decision, and the naive filmic curves were already measured and rejected — plus a sky in frame.
  • A green rather than an olive. Even after L3 the meadow's blue channel is 0.21 against the reference's 0.50. That is the same fault as the shadow one, at the albedo instead of the light.
  • Truly blue shadows. b−r dark improves from −0.087 to −0.056 but never reaches reference 1's +0.090. It cannot, while the terrain's own maps are brown at source: COLOR_0 multiplies a warm texture and can only make it less warm. Genuinely blue shade needs the terrain textures re-authored cooler, which is an art pass, not a probe.
  • Rock strata as GEOMETRY. L3 gives the cliff tonal bedding; it does not give it the reference's ledges, overhangs and fracture relief. Those are modelled, and modelling a 60 m gorge wall properly is its own tranche.
  • Grass at reference density. Reference 1's meadow is thousands of blades per square metre with alpha cards and wind. Ours is six triangles per tuft at seams only. It is the right STRUCTURE at a fraction of the density, and it will always be legible as such at a stare.
  • The character's contact shadow. Every reference puts a clean soft pool under its character. Ours has one, but it is a 2048‑texel map spread over 230 m; it is soft because it is low‑resolution, not because it is a good shadow. A small second cascade around the body would fix it and is not in this probe.

Method, and the two traps this lane paid for

Every plate is a real frame from the shipped runtime at ?scene=ow-valley&rt=1&owsky=1&owlight=1, captured over CDP by tools/ow_probe/land.js through tools/ow_probe/ow_multi.mjs — one Chrome launch, 32 frames. Blender was deliberately not used: ow-valley renders in real time under play3d's own rig, so a Blender still would be a picture of a different lighting model. Numbers are tools/ow_probe/imgstat.py. The bundle, the map files and play3d.html were not modified.

1. r128 FRUSTUM-CULLS AN InstancedMesh AGAINST THE BASE GEOMETRY'S BOUNDING
   SPHERE, transformed by the OBJECT's matrixWorld — the per-instance matrices
   are not in it.  A scatter batch whose base form is a 0.15 m tuft at the origin
   is therefore culled from every camera that cannot see world (0,0,0), and
   28,347 tufts rendered as EXACTLY NOTHING while every count in the report was
   correct.  Two rounds were spent raising the density of something that was
   being drawn perfectly and thrown away.  A REPORT THAT SAYS "28,347 PLACED" AND
   A FRAME THAT SHOWS NONE IS A CULLING BUG, NEVER A PLACEMENT BUG.
   frustumCulled = false, or give the batch a real bounding sphere.

2. r128 SILENTLY DROPS instanceColor WHEN material.vertexColors IS FALSE.
   color_fragment only applies vColor under USE_COLOR, and USE_COLOR comes from
   material.vertexColors — so instance colours vanish.  Set vertexColors:true and
   the geometry must ALSO carry a color attribute, or the missing attribute reads
   as zero and every instance renders black.  An all-ones COLOR_0 on the base
   form plus vertexColors:true is the only combination that is right.

3. THE SLOPE GATE IS NOT OPTIONAL for seam-driven scatter.  The seam test fires
   HARDEST exactly where grass meets vertical rock, so the first L2 grew grass
   straight out of 60 m of gorge wall.  Reject on the local gradient of the
   terrain sampler, not on the zone.

4. SCALE IS AGAINST THE BODY.  Vesper is 1.45 u.  The first tufts were 0.4-1.0 u
   and the shelf grew a hedge of black agave; the reference's grass is ankle-to-
   shin on its character, so 0.10-0.34.  And an instance colour is a multiplier
   ON THE MAP'S OWN MEAN, not on 1.0: ow_valley_bushcore ships COLOR_0 at L 0.311,
   so tinting a clump at 0.5-0.8 produced faceted BLACK BOULDERS at every rock
   foot.  x2.4 puts it back in the band the shipped bush masses occupy.

INHERITED AND STILL TRUE: three r128 has no input colour management (an injected
hex renders chalk without convertSRGBToLinear, and values read back OUT of
COLOR_0 are already linear); SIM.tp(x,z) raycasts from the CURRENT P.y; the
terrain and vegetation meshes are UNINDEXED; and no instrument can derive a road
heading from the RLE-ordered zone raster — nothing here needs one, the road is
used only as a MASK.
