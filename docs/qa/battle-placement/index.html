Is this a good place to fight?

2026-08-09 · the world arena's staging search, measured for the first time on the one thing it never asked · instruments tools/battle_place.mjs, tools/battle_place_sheets.py, tools/battle_place_stats.py

stageArena stages 100% of sampled ow-valley cells and the camera language owns size, occlusion and tonal separation. The places it picks are another matter: it staged on a bare rock dome, in a building's shadow, inside a hedge bank, on a rooftop, and once with the lens inside a leaf. All of those are legal placements. The search only ever asked can bodies stand here and can the camera see them.

Method — the sort came first, on purpose

62 sites were staged as real battles through the shipped path and photographed with the stage's own snapshot(), so every frame below is the player's pipeline (RenderPass → GTAO → bloom → OutputPass) and the bytes are display-space. They were then sorted by eye into good / acceptable / bad from contact sheets alone, before a single number was read (sort.json; sheets in sheets/). Only then was every recorded axis asked how well it separates the piles. A metric invented before the sort is taste with a number on it.

The 62 sites are close to a census, not a sample: walking all 400 recorded road cells into the neighbouring encounter zones and refusing any site within 5 m of an accepted one exhausts at 62. That is the size of the band a fight can actually fire in beside the ow-valley road.

What won

Whether the frame is one flat lit thing or many. surf.topSurface — the share of the background frame owned by the single most common quantised colour — separates at AUC 0.922 (good 0.199, bad 0.444), and its entropy twin agrees. And whether the cast stands in the sun. sun.shadedFrac reads 0.39 at good sites and 0.86 at bad ones (AUC 0.157 inverted = 0.843), and it costs four rays where the surface term costs a render.

What did not — the negative results, which cost more to learn

  • Sky / horizon presence is not the axis. rays.skyFrac reads AUC 0.681 and rays.horizonRow 0.556. Almost no frame in this valley contains sky at all; the camera pitch band and the valley walls see to that.
  • The axis the solver already optimises barely separates. view.back — clear depth behind each body, the whole argument the base pose is searched on — reads AUC 0.733, and view.seen reads 0.657 because it is ~0.99 everywhere by construction. The staging solver is already maximising something that is not the thing that matters.
  • Relocation distance predicts nothing. site.R / site.d: AUC 0.450. Where the ladder puts the fight relative to the player has no bearing on whether it reads — which independently supports the 2026-08-08 ruling that distance is not the constraint.
  • Ground slope is weak (AUC 0.318 → sep 0.364); roughness and vertical span are moderate (sep 0.510 / 0.678). Terrain flatness is a minor term, not the story.
  • And the shipped tonal metric runs backwards across sites. sil.edgeRGB — what the TONE boom chooser maximises — ranks the three piles at 0.291 ordered-pair concordance: good sites average 8.9 against bad sites' 17.3. A lit body against near-black is enormous RGB contrast and is exactly the pile a human calls bad. TONE is not wrong at its own job — choosing a boom at one site — but it must never be used as a placement metric.
  • Combining does not help. Sun exposure and surface dominance correlate 0.59; the z-sum of the two scores AUC 0.881, worse than surface dominance alone at 0.922.

The change

The ladder returned on its first acceptance, and ring 0 accepts at most cells — so there was never a second candidate to prefer and any quality term would have been inert. It now finishes the ring that first accepted, walks one more, and prefers a materially sunnier site among them. Sun exposure is the term, not surface dominance: the render-based winner has no affordable proxy (its ray-grid stand-in scores only AUC 0.79 and costs 52 ms a candidate — measured, proxy-8x5.json), and sun exposure separates nearly as well for four rays.

It is a refusal, not a preference — the same shape TONE's margin has. A challenger must move at least half the cast out of shadow (PLACE.sunMargin = 0.5) before it takes the site; the incumbent is still the ladder's own first acceptance and is never assumed to be the worst. ?bplace=0 is the A/B on one build.

Two short-circuits, proven rather than argued. lit is a fraction, so if the incumbent is already within the margin of fully lit no candidate can clear the bar (22/62 cells); and candidates are generated in ring-then-bearing order with the tie-break sunniest→nearest→earliest, so the first fully-lit challenger that clears the margin is exactly what the ranking would pick (19/62). tools/battle_place.mjs --mode=equiv drives both arms in ONE page with ORBIT.yaw pinned — 62/62 identical sites, 0 differ — and the fast path cuts the walk from 72.9 ms to 46.9 ms at p50. The yaw pin matters: solveArena's ladder is relative to the live camera heading, so two separate runs of the same cell are not comparable and six cells appeared to differ for exactly that reason.

Where default-readiness stands

One site in three is still a bad place to fight. The 20 that remain bad are the ones sun exposure cannot reach: a frame that is one flat surface edge to edge (the rock domes s008 / s018), a place with no sunlit neighbour inside 8 m (the deep-shadow cluster s051 / s052 / s054), the interiors under structures (s003 / s032 / s040), and the rooftop (s026). Making ?arena=world the default today ships a fight that reads well at one site in four and badly at one in three. The measured next lever is the axis that won and was not shipped — surface dominance — which needs either an affordable proxy nobody has found yet or a render budget the staging path does not have.
