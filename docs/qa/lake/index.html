Verdict — RE-ROLL

Two independent blockers, and the second is not fixed by re-downloading.

  1. The file is corrupt. Its last 2,624 bytes — which are exactly the whole inverseBindMatrices bufferView, the last thing in the buffer — are a byte-identical copy of the file's own first 2,624 bytes. A chunked/resumed download wrote a retry at the wrong offset. Because Tripo wrote 0 of 41 joint nodes with any transform, the ENTIRE skeleton lived in that block. Mesh, weights and textures are intact; the rig is gone. Blender imports it without an error and gives 41 bones whose every head is NaN.
  2. The cape is skinned to the arms and the legs. That is a property of the Tripo job, not of the transfer, so the same job re-downloaded returns the same weights.

What that does to a walk

Synthetic demonstration, not a bake — there is no rig left to bake. Linear-blend skinning applied by group weight about shoulder/hip pivots estimated from the mesh. It shows what any arm swing does to geometry carrying arm-bone weight.

The good news, so the re-roll keeps it

The arms are not fused into the cape. Cross-sectioning the mesh every 2% of height, Lake's arms read as separate closed loops from 42% up to 52% and merge into the torso section at 54% — the same height Finn's do (Finn: separate to 50%, merged at 54%; Vesper, whose arms are held further out, stays separate to 62%). Both hands are clean 100%-hand regions of the same size as Vesper's. The user-ratified decision to sweep the cape BACK in the turnaround did its job: it bought the arms their silhouette. The defect is the auto-rigger's proximity binding on a garment that hangs 33% of body height away from the spine, not the pose.
