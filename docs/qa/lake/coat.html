Lake — the coat redesign

2026-08-02 · why the cape had to go, what replaced it, and the turnaround that is ready for Tripo

The short version. The first Tripo delivery failed for two independent reasons. The file itself arrived corrupt — a resumed download wrote a retry at byte zero, overwriting exactly the block that holds the skeleton's bind matrices. That alone is just a re-download. The blocker is the second one: the cape bound to his arms and legs, not his spine. An auto-rigger binds each vertex to the nearest joint, and the cape hung about 35 cm clear of the body, where nothing nearby is the spine. Under a stride it scissored with the limbs. The fix keeps everything that was working and closes the garment against the body.

The measurement that decided it

Cape panel A came back 85.9% arm-weighted; panel B 67.1% arm / 27.7% leg. For scale, the largest sheet on any shipped model is Vesper's collar at 1,610 verts.

The swept-back decision was vindicated in the same pass — the arms were genuinely separate geometry (closed loops at 42–52%, matching Finn) and both hands bound 100% clean. So the pose stays. Only the garment changes.

Round 1 — three directions

Generated as full A-poses, because that is the view that has to bind. Two findings, both from looking rather than from any metric: only one roll held the house render style, and the two "open front" variants reproduce the cape defect in miniature.

Round 2 — refining the pick

Three rolls chained off A-1 asking for a narrower skirt that falls straight against the legs. The model refused the edit — all three came back essentially identical. Kept here because the null result is the point: it stopped the iteration. On reflection the flare does not matter. A skirt binding to the legs is a skirt behaving like a skirt; the cape's fatal error was binding to the arms.

Shipped — the turnaround for Tripo

Front is A-1 rendered directly in the house style; left, right and back are chained off it, so all four views share one anchor rather than averaging two references. (That mattered: the previous attempt blended a stale pre-redesign ref and returned a genuinely different face.)

One honest reservation. In the side views his hands sit close to the coat skirt. If the mesh fuses them, the intake gate catches it in about twenty seconds and the front view — which dominates the conditioning and has clear separation — is the one to re-roll.

What did not change

The cut-in portraits keep the front-draped cape, deliberately. A portrait never animates. A portrait and a walk cycle want different silhouettes, and the dramatic drape is what makes his close-ups read.
