R5 improved the number and made the picture worse. Lit:shadow went 1.58–2.40 → 3.29–4.33 and a blind critic still ranked r5-meadow LAST of four: “this is a global tint, not a light.” Both readings were true. The KEY had a direction; the GRADE did not, and the blade carpet that arrived in the same window was rendering half its instances black. R6 keeps every bit of R5's directional gain and fixes what it was applied to.

Going the rest of the way was tried and it looked worse. A key at (1.0, 0.90, 0.74) with the grade tint at 1.0 lands meadow NEAR at 0.639 and gate at 0.585 — closer to the target on every row — and the frame stops being golden hour. The trees flatten, the picture goes milky, the amber is graded AWAY rather than into the light. Refused under the standing ruling: “do not grade the amber away; grade it into the light.” r6 sits at 0.60–0.70. The remaining gap is the ground's own albedo (the terrain's COLOR_0 grass was deliberately pushed to a strong yellow-green), which is a CONTENT change, not a grade one, and is the honest next lever.

What actually changed

1 · the warm half of the grade is now weighted by light

R5 multiplied every near pixel by warmCol — a global tint by definition, arriving through the post pass instead of through the ambient, which is the exact defect the lighting round existed to remove. The warm push is now scaled by the pixel's own luminance (litLo 0.16 → litHi 0.46): full on a lit surface, nothing in a shadow. The COOL far push is deliberately not weighted, because haze is atmospheric and does land on everything. Tint magnitude 2.2 → 1.45.

2 · the key's blue channel is opened

(1.0, 0.70, 0.42) at 2.4× multiplies a grass albedo of roughly (0.35, 0.55, 0.20) into (2.2, 2.4, 0.5) — yellow. No lit surface in the valley could read as green whatever its albedo. Now (1.0, 0.785, 0.555): still unmistakably golden hour, still warmer than either reference, but a green albedo comes back green.

3 · the blade carpet was rendering half its instances BLACK

The user read this as “hard black alpha edges”. There is no alpha in the blade material at all — it is solid geometry. The black is three.js's DOUBLE_SIDED normal flip: the blade's authored up-normal (0, 0.94, 0.34) becomes (0, −0.94, −0.34) on a back face, pointing at the dirt, receiving neither key nor sky. Blade yaw is uniform over 2π, so ~half of 177k instances were down-facing. Un-flipped in onBeforeCompile. The tilt itself was also measured and was the speckle: 0.34 of lateral normal swings a blade's own N·L from 0.24 to 0.81 depending on which way it happens to be yawed, and put the AVERAGE blade at 0.29 against the ground's 0.56 — dark scribble over lit ground. Now 0.14. Height 0.19–0.42 → 0.085–0.235 (ankle height on a 1.45u character), power-distributed instead of uniform, with per-instance hue as well as value jitter, two octaves of world-space clumping so the field has bare ground in it, and receiveShadow on so the carpet stops being brighter than the ground inside every tree shadow.

4 · the Heartlight has a core and a falloff

A uniformly emissive sphere is a flat disc on screen by construction, and R5 could only lower the emissive of the material the orb SHARES with fourteen window panes. It now carries an opaque amber body that covers the bundle's clipping ico, a small hot additive core, and a drawn radial glow — bloom alone gives a halo whose profile is the blur kernel's, which is why R5 had “a hard-ish boundary”. The point light was measured reaching under 2% of its plinth value at the nearest houses; at 26 W / range 26 it now puts +9.4 red on the nearest house and +8.8 on the character, with the inverse-square falloff untouched.

Two bugs paid for on the way, both recorded in the source. A shader patch that replaced float faceDirection = gl_FrontFacing ? 1.0 : − 1.0; matched NOTHING — onBeforeCompile runs BEFORE resolveIncludes, so the hook is handed #include <normal_fragment_begin>, not the expanded chunk. It would have shipped as a no-op inside a change that looked better anyway. And a custom-material mesh built at load time rendered THE WHOLE FRAME BLACK — 0.97/255 against 115/255, no console error, no shader warning, no exception, rAF still ticking. Bisected to ?bloom_s=0 restoring the frame; the orb is built from stock materials instead.

Still worse than r4

  • L05 is lower on every view (meadow 0.216 → 0.155, gorge 0.207 → 0.063 against the references' 0.21–0.25). That is R5's cost, carried forward deliberately — it is the price of the terminator and the frames read contrasty rather than grim. ?owenv= raises the fill without touching anything else.
  • Near-band saturation is still 0.60–0.70 against r4's 0.29–0.51, see above.
  • The gorge's two grey rectangles over the sky were not touched here — they are gone in the plates because a concurrent g-buffer lane fixed them (see PROVENANCE at the top).
  • The near field is less present than r5 wanted it to be: halving the blade height and adding bare ground buys legibility and costs some of the "you are standing in grass" read at the 12 m meadow boom. It is the right trade at this camera and it is a trade.
