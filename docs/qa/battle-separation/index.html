TONAL SEPARATION — does the cast read against real terrain?

2026-08-09 · world arena only (?arena=world) · instruments tools/battle_sep.mjs + the shared meter tools/battle_meter.mjs · board for the last measured gap between the world arena and being good enough to make default.

The camera lane's verdict, which this board exists to answer: “the world wins on picture and loses on legibility — two clean silhouettes on a low-contrast painted field is a composition; the valley is rock, foliage, houses and cast shadows.” The shot language moved worst-case foe height 14.6% → 18.8% of frame and left RGB silhouette contrast where it found it.

0 · How it is measured, and why that is not negotiable

Three meter rules this repo has already paid for, inherited whole from tools/battle_camera.mjs — which now imports the same file this tool does (tools/battle_meter.mjs), because a meter that is copy-pasted is two meters that drift:

  • a silhouette is a subtraction — the frame with a body minus the frame without it, shadow and alpha and all — never a heuristic about where the body is;
  • occlusion is an intersection with the depth-test-off silhouette, never a ratio of areas (GTAO and bloom leave halos the two passes do not share; a fully visible body once measured “−136% occluded”);
  • contrast is RGB, never luminance — the cutin_edge lesson, where a chartreuse fringe sat on 79 of 112 shipped plates, every one gate-green, because the gate measured brightness and a rim of the wrong hue at the right brightness is invisible to it.

edgeRGB = RGB distance between a body's own edge band and the background ring around it. edgeMin = the worst body in the frame. clutter = the ring's own luminance spread. snr = edgeRGB ÷ clutter — because contrast alone is not legibility: a body 20 units from a background whose own variance is 46 does not read.

4 · WHAT SHIPPED — the boom is chosen on tone, in pixels

public/js/battle_world.js, TONE. Once the cast has arrived, the game runs the meter's own subtraction on itself: per candidate boom, render the arena into a 320×180 offscreen target without the cast and again with it; the difference is each body's silhouette. Score is the RGB distance between a body's edge pixels and the background ring around them, half the mean and half the minimum — the worst-placed body is the one that fails to read — minus the ring's clutter.

  • It writes no shader and moves no page state. An offscreen render target and a temporary camera; the page's camera, ORBIT, canvas and post chain are untouched, so nothing flickers and nothing has to be restored.
  • It is a screen, not a verdict. The probe renders straight — no GTAO, no bloom, no aerial grade — so it ranks; battle_sep.mjs measuring the real pipeline is what judges.
  • SAY WHICH SPACE THE BYTES ARE IN. r185 renders into a non-XR target in the linear working space whatever the texture declares, and the OutputPass is not in this loop — so the probe applies exactly one explicit sRGB encode through a 256-entry table before it differences anything. This is the conversion the r185 rules ask for, not the double convertSRGBToLinear they warn about: the renderer performed none. It was measured wrong first: ranking raw linear bytes crushes a dark site and picked the wrong boom at the forest (edgeRGB 12.88 → 11.11). Tone mapping is still not applied, and that is the probe's stated approximation.
  • It is a REFUSAL, not a preference. The probe may only overrule the geometric solver when it beats the incumbent rung's score by 35%. Without that margin the same build picked different rungs at forest and crag between two runs — inside the 15–24% band the probe's ranking is not reliable enough to act on. The margin turns every marginal call into today's game and keeps the one decisive call.
  • It only ever lands on the opening. It waits for every body to leave the proxy tier (a proxy solid is the wrong colour to rank a background against) and applies its answer only while the opening round shot is still easing, so it reads as one continuous move. If a beat already owns the camera the answer is recorded and not applied.

Cost measured: 36.9 / 41.9 / 56.1 / 62.2 ms, once per battle, against a shipped trigger-to-first-battle-frame of p50 688 ms. ?btone=0 is the A/B on one build.

8 · WHAT STILL STANDS — and what a shader would buy

  • The forest is flat and no camera fixes it. Measured across all five boom rungs, the worst body's |bodyL − ringL| is 0.1–1.7 and the whole site's edgeRGB band is 10.9–13.5. The cast and the canopy are the same value at the same saturation.
  • The meadow's background is the busiest in the game (clutter 44–50 against water's 17–27) and the boom cannot move it, because the clutter is the ground scatter the player walks through.
  • Both are body-side problems, and the body side is where the shader is. A cast-only rim is unavailable as a light — three tests a light's layers against the CAMERA, never per object — so it is a fresnel through onBeforeCompile, which would be this module's first shader. If a later lane reaches for it, the guarantees it reopens are named: (1) the grade must convert to display space explicitly, (2) no hand convertSRGBToLinear anywhere near it, and (3) IU() untouched. The non-shader alternative with the same reach is a precomputed inverted-hull shell (expanded positions on a cloned geometry, BackSide, MeshBasicMaterial, skeleton shared) — a graphic outline rather than a light, and a look decision rather than a fix.

10 · IS THE WORLD ARENA READY TO BE THE DEFAULT?

On tonal separation: not yet — but the thing standing in the way is no longer the camera, and it is no longer the case that “the plates are more legible than the valley” across the board.

What changed: the meadow regression the arc has been carrying does not exist on this tree, and the water one is fixed (+63% contrast, +86% on the worst body, +163% snr). Two of four sites now read.

What still stands, named precisely:

  1. FOREST. The party stages inside the hedge bank at the hedge's own value. Worst-body |bodyL − ringL| is 0.1–1.7 at every one of five boom rungs. Not a camera problem.
  2. MEADOW. Background clutter 44–50, roughly double every other site, and it is the ground scatter the player walks through — the camera cannot move it and the aerial cue cannot reach it (§6). The party also stages into a building's shadow.
  3. CRAG. The arena staged on a bare rock dome; the fight is legible and the picture is not. A placement-quality term (is this a good place to fight, not merely a legal one) is the missing lever, and nobody has measured it.
  4. The body side is untouched. Every lever this lane shipped is a camera lever. The two remaining failures are body-versus-near-background coincidences, and the instruments that would close them are (e) a fresnel rim — this module's first shader — or a precomputed inverted-hull shell. Both are look decisions and neither has been costed by eye.

Recommendation: the world arena is close enough that the remaining work is placement quality and cast lighting, not arena choice. Making it default now ships a fight that reads at two of four sampled sites and is muddy at the other two — that is a call for the user, and the honest input to it is that the diorama's advantage was never the arena, it was that a painted plate is a background somebody composed.
