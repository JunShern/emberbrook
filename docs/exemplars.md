# EMBERBROOK — the exemplars

**What this is.** The ratified style set: real shipped lines from Chapters One and Two
that already do what `docs/VOICES.md` asks. VOICES.md *describes* the house style;
this file *is* the house style. Match by example, not by paraphrasing the abstract.

**How to use it.**
- Writing a new scene? Read the speaker's block first and write toward the rhythm.
- Generating dialogue with a model? Paste PART 2 as the few-shot block. It is written
  to be pasted: speaker, line, and one sentence on why the line works.
- Judging a draft? If a new line would look out of place next to these, it is out of
  place. Do not argue with the file; rewrite the line.

**The rules these lines obey**, all of them measured by `node tools/dialogue_style.mjs`:
two sentences per box · 8–18 words (25 hard) · plain first, flourish second ·
one capped word per box · one aphorism per scene, from a licensed voice.
Every line below passes that gate. If you change a line here, change it in the
chapter too — an exemplar that no longer ships is a lie in a reference file.

Scope note (user ruling, 2026-08-02): exemplars come from Chapters One and Two only.
`chapter3.js` is not the house style and none of its lines appear here.

## PART 1 — the five things every exemplar below is doing

1. **The fact is in the first sentence.** Flavor, if it earns a place, comes second.
   Nobody in this file makes the player wait for the point.
2. **The voice is in the rhythm, not the vocabulary.** Odessa is recognisable because
   her sentences *end*; Maren because hers double back; Vesper because hers are filed.
   None of them needs an unusual word to be themselves.
3. **A long speech is a stack of short boxes.** Where the shipped script had a wall,
   the rewrite has three boxes and a beat. Split, never gut.
4. **The feeling is stated once, plainly, and then dropped.** "I'm scared to walk that
   road, and I'll walk it." No one explains it afterwards.
5. **The joke is the character, not the writer.** Rowan punctures himself; Pell
   defends the dignity of ladders; Poppy legislates bread. The narrator never winks.

## PART 2 — the exemplars

### VESPER — mapmaker, co-lead
*Precise, quick, dry. Feelings arrive filed as paperwork.*

- **"Rules can be charted. Charting things is my whole profession."**
  Her entire thesis in nine words, and it answers an elder's "it has RULES" without
  raising her voice. Plain sentence, plain sentence, done.
- **"Nowhere, yet. It’s under survey. Ask me again when the survey’s done."**
  The deflection that defines her: homelessness re-filed as an open work item. The
  player sees straight through it, which is the point — she doesn't yet.
- **"…Around. Professionally around."**
  Three words. The armour, and exactly how thin it is, in the same breath. Proof that
  the shortest box in a scene can be the loudest.
- **"It comes back. That’s in ink now."**
  A promise, made the only way she knows how to make one. Never say "I promise" when
  a record-keeper can say "ink".
- **"For the record: this is now the best chart I carry."**
  Notebook liturgy spent as tenderness — the joke and the kindness are the same words.
  This is what her tics are FOR.

### LAKE — the last lamplighter, co-lead
*Plain and warm. Few words, all load-bearing. Names the obvious thing everyone is
talking around.*

- **"On duty, Poppy."**
  Three words that decline a honeybun, state a job, and stay affectionate. He drops
  the subject whenever the sentence still works without it.
- **"You’re Poppy. You bake."**
  His superpower, at its smallest: a person and their one true detail. After the Hush
  this exact move is the whole counter-attack.
- **"Kept a lamp. Same wrist."**
  Answering a compliment by shrinking it. Five words, one shrug, and a lifetime of
  trade in the second half.
- **"If a town can be fine with no flame at all — what’s a keeper for?"**
  Chapter Two's thesis, asked out loud instead of brooded internally (Rule D4).
  Fifteen words, no mysticism, no metaphor.
- **"I’m scared to walk that road, and I’ll walk it. Both of those are true."**
  Emotion in the clear (Rule D3). Says the fear plainly, then refuses to resolve it.
  Wit may follow a plain feeling; here nothing does, and it lands harder.

### MAREN — 17, pilot
*Fast, loud, self-interrupting — until she is on the water, where she goes clipped.*

- **"Mind the forty-first step, it lies."**
  Hospitality disguised as a warning, in six words of pure lock-town. Her whole
  relationship with a stranger, established before anyone has been introduced.
- **"…Ten. The point stands!"**
  The count correction, conceded and then instantly overridden. Her mother is right
  and it changes nothing: that is the family in four words.
- **"It’s not sad. It’s bookkeeping."**
  Refusing the sentimental reading of her own tally arm. Note the rhythm — she takes
  the feeling away from you before you can offer it.
- **"Oars in. Weight low. Nobody row — we’re guests."**
  On the water the run-on stops dead. Three commands and a joke that is also a rule.
  Same character, opposite instrument.
- **"(quiet) Beat your time, Da. You’d have hated that."** → *next box* →
  **"…You’d have loved that."**
  Two boxes, because the second thought has to arrive after the first one has landed.
  This is the model for every "long speech becomes a sequence of short boxes."

### ODESSA — guildmother of the locks
*Short imperatives. Never explains. The warmth is entirely in what her hands do.*

- **"Wash. Basin’s by the door."**
  Five words: an order, a courtesy, and a whole hospitality. Subject, verb, done.
- **"The river IS the road. The road is shut."**
  A ruling stated as physics. One capped word, placed where a voice actor would
  actually punch it, and never repeated.
- **"…Mind the beam when you shout. You’ll smudge your father’s marks."**
  "Mind the—" is care disguised as instruction, and here it is also the answer to the
  question her daughter just asked. She does not acknowledge that it was.
- **"…That bar is cut for six hands. Move over."**
  Permission, apology and participation, none of them named. She joins the crime her
  daughter is committing and calls it arithmetic.
- **"The no was what I owed my own heart. The bag is what I owed yours."**
  Her one aphorism for the chapter (§2 budget), spent at the peak, while her hands are
  full. Nobody remarks on it afterwards, which is what makes it survivable.

### ROWAN — elder and ledger-keeper
*Old-fashioned courtesy worn soft, funny at his own expense, bookkeeping for imagery.*

- **"In charge! Ha! I keep the ledger; the village keeps itself."**
  Deflects authority, gets the laugh, and states the town's actual politics — all in
  one box, with the "Ha!" doing the work an exclamation mark can't.
- **"Ink keeps facts. It cannot keep what they WEIGHED."**
  The metaphysics of the whole game, said by a man holding a book. No mysticism: he is
  describing his own job and happens to be describing the universe.
- **"That is not boasting, my dear. It is bookkeeping — I checked."**
  His tic ("…I checked") landing as a punchline on a genuinely proud claim. The
  em-dash is doing the work a third sentence used to do.
- **"…The cat is going with you."**
  Six words that hand the party a companion and refuse to discuss it.
- **"It was not a question, boy. Some decisions are made over our heads."**
  Tradition register (§7): total confidence, cheerfully no explanation offered. He is
  not being mysterious; he genuinely does not think it needs one.

### POPPY — baker
*Bossy kitchen warmth. Feeding people is legislation.*

- **"Chart the— HA! Did you hear her? Eat two."**
  Interrupts herself, mocks nobody, and closes with an order that is also a gift.
  Every Poppy line should be able to end on food, and most of them do.
- **"Half a bun. I’ll hold the other half hostage until the Kindling Hour."**
  Hospitality as leverage. She never lets anyone leave hungry and never says so.
- **"Strangest rule your family ever kept, love. Saddest one, too."**
  Ten words that hand Lake his whole arc, from a woman who is not making a speech
  about it — she is putting bread down at the same time.
- **"Good, love. Write the thumb. The thumb feels true."**
  Post-Hush: the command voice, aimed at her own rescue. Concrete, small, and much
  worse than any abstract statement of loss would be.
- **"Honeybuns. Poppy. Thumb. I’m keeping the words in a row where I can see them."**
  Three nouns and a plan. She rebuilds herself out of a shopping list, and the box
  never tells you how to feel about it.

## PART 3 — supporting voices

### PIP — seven
- **"It’s the BEST stick. You can hold it for one minute."** — total conviction, one
  idea per sentence, and a favour granted with a time limit.
- **"She’s been to the moon."** — declares a thing settled on no evidence. Instant,
  irreversible canon.
- **"…Is it in ink?"** — post-Hush. Four words, and the entire endgame's mechanism,
  asked by the only person young enough to just believe the answer.

### FINN — fisherman
- **"Didn’t think so either, this morning."** — the flattest possible delivery of the
  scariest fact in the chapter. His sentences shorten as things get stranger.
- **"They’re swimmin’ in a circle — one big circle, all of ’em. Slow."** — one marker
  (dropped g's), used once, and an escalation that ends on a single word.
- **"Finn. Still short. Still like it."** — post-Hush, and he has decided to be fine
  about it. Nobody asked him to be brave; he is being laconic instead.

### THE QUAY — one line each, all nameplate-test-proof
- **Hobb:** "Nineteen days of my wife saying pie." — big feelings arrive as logistics.
- **Pell:** "It is presently day, which is why I'm holding a wick-knife and a grudge."
  — the standing joke states his whole trade and his whole grievance at once.
- **Sorrel:** "Half-loaf's still a penny. The cat's credit is good." — prices
  everything, including things that are not for sale. That IS the affection.
- **Creel:** "Mind your feet going down. Coming up, mind everything else." — four
  hundred years of stairs, delivered as a handrail.
- **Nib:** "Are you the flame people? You're littler than the quay said." — no filter
  between observation and mouth, and he is appraising you, not admiring you.
- **Mara:** "He'll fall asleep mid-sentence and deny it mid-fall." — steady,
  unsentimental, funny without ever performing the funniness.

### THE NARRATOR — `narrate` cards
- **"It did not happen slowly. Between one heartbeat and the next, the light of
  Emberbrook — three hundred years of it — stood up and left."**
  Fact first, one image, and the image is a verb. The card never tells you it is sad.
- **"They walked until the last light of Emberbrook was gone behind them, and slept
  badly under a bramble."**
  A whole night of travel and grief in one sentence, carried entirely by "badly".

### THE SYSTEM CHANNEL — stage directions, examine text, and the cat
- **"(She refills Maren’s bowl before Maren notices it’s empty. That is the answer.)"**
  The model for the whole channel: cut a relationship down to the one physical action
  that shows it, then stop. Thirteen words.
- **"(A long negotiation occurs, at the speed of cat. Mochi boards the satchel facing
  backward, as if the whole thing were his idea and everyone else were late.)"**
  Officialese reporting observable conduct. Never translate the cat's thoughts; report
  what he did and let the grammar imply the rest.
- **"(Mochi eats on the step — the WARM half of the step. Odessa pretends not to
  notice.)"**
  Two people's characters, one cat, no adjectives about anybody's feelings.

## PART 4 — the anti-patterns these replaced

Kept short on purpose. Each is a real shape the 2026-08-02 pass removed.

| Shape | Why it went |
|---|---|
| Three or four sentences in one box | The player reads at walking speed; the third sentence is where they start skimming. Split it. |
| A fact wrapped in a flourish | "The water claimed its due" makes the player decode before they can act. Say the fact, then flavour it — or don't. |
| Two aphorisms in one scene | The second one costs the first one all its weight. Keep the higher peak; write the other one flat. |
| An internal `(…)` line carrying a plot fact | If the player must know it, someone says it out loud (Rule D4). Internals carry feeling *about* facts. |
| A system box explaining a relationship | Cut to the single action that shows it. If there is no action, the box is not needed. |
| Exclamation-point padding | Excitement is a short sentence, not a punctuation mark. |
