1 · The workflow — sheet to screen

Every object in the prototype goes through the same five steps. The images below are the actual files on disk, not illustrations.

2 · The block inspector — every block, with its metadata drawn on top

One card per entry in registry.js. The sprite sits on a checkerboard; on top of it we draw what the metadata claims: the footprint diamonds (red = blocks walking, green = you can walk there), the anchor point the renderer pins to the ground (yellow cross + dotted line), and the lowest painted pixel of the art itself (white dashed line). If the art and the metadata disagree, you will see it here — a sprite whose painted feet end above its yellow anchor line will float in game. An automatic check compares the two lines and badges any mismatch.

3 · The scene X-ray

Each scene rendered by the engine's real draw path (same ground bake, same block placement, same painter's sort, character at spawn), with the metadata layers drawn on top. Toggle layers with the checkboxes. Beside it, the same camera with the art removed — metadata only. Comparing the two is where placement bugs become obvious: a prop drawn over a roof, or a painted wall with no red cells under it.

4 · Glossary

the 0.5 m cell
The world is a grid of half-metre squares. Each cell is drawn as a 64×32 px diamond (the classic 2:1 isometric shape). All placement, collision and sorting is done in these cell coordinates (i, j); the character moves smoothly between cells but collides against whole cells.
anchor
The one pixel of a sprite that the renderer pins to a point on the ground. For "free" props it is the centre of the object's painted base (ax/ay in the registry), pinned to the centre of its footprint. For "slab" and building sprites it is the bottom tip of the image, pinned to the footprint's front corner. If the anchor is measured wrong — or missing — the art hovers or sinks.
footprint
The rectangle of cells a block claims on the ground (foot: [w, h]). It is both the visual "ground it occupies" and the default collision area.
solid subset
Optionally, only some footprint cells actually block walking (solid: [[i,j],…]). A tree claims 4×4 cells so its canopy has room, but only the 2×2 trunk cells are solid — you walk under the leaves. Red vs green on every card shows this split.
sort key
Draw order. Each prop gets one number, i + j + (footW + footH)/2; props are drawn from smallest to largest, and the character is slotted into that order with key x + y. Bigger key = closer to the camera. It is a one-number approximation — good enough for small props, and exactly the thing that breaks when footprints are large or overlap (see the X-ray's "sort keys" layer).
supertile vs free prop
Ground comes as 2×2-cell supertiles stamped from a character map in the scene JSON ("g" = grass…), baked once into a seamless floor. Everything that stands on the floor is a free prop (or slab/backdrop variant): an independent transparent sprite anchored by its base point and sorted per frame.
kinds: ground / flat / free / slab / backdrop
ground: floor material, baked, never sorted. flat: floor decal (rug), drawn after floor, walkable. free: standing prop, sorted every frame. slab: prop whose image bottom-tip anchors to the footprint's front corner. backdrop: building facades and room shells — drawn before all sorted props, i.e. always behind the character and behind every prop. That is why a prop placed behind a building can still draw on top of its roof.
tall sprites
A sprite may be far taller than its footprint (a 2×2 tree is 365 px tall) — that is fine and normal for this style. The height only works if the sort is right: the sprite is just a billboard standing on its footprint, so anything with a bigger key must be drawn later, and backdrops must genuinely be behind everything placed in front of them.

In the prototype itself (iso-proto.html) press G — same key as the main game's walkability overlay — to see the grid, walkable colouring, footprints and anchors live under your feet while you walk.
