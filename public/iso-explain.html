The registration mark — anchors by construction

Every prop on this round's sheet was generated standing on a flat magenta registration mark shaped as its declared footprint (2:1 iso diamond for 1×1, strip for 2×1, square for 2×2; one cell = 0.5 m), on a bare light-grey background — no terrain, no shadows. The mark is the measurement: the slicer keys the magenta, fits the mark's parallelogram, and reads off the anchor (mark centre), the ground contact patch, and the per-sprite cell size — no hand-tuned ax/ay anywhere. Verdict of this round: the convention works as designed for diamond and square footprints — six of nine props measured at 0.97–1.00 confidence with base spill under 2 px, and their art sits exactly in its claimed footprint. The three 2×1 strip footprints are the failure case: across all six generations the image model refused to paint a true 2:1 two-cell strip (it squares it, or paints it elongated but mirrored — principal axis ≈ −21° where the declared strip lies at +26.6°), so bench / planter / firewood register on a pad that does not match their declaration — the mark measures this precisely, which is the point: bad registration is now detected, not discovered in-scene.
