The registration mark — anchors by construction

Every prop on this round's sheet was generated standing on a flat magenta registration mark shaped as its declared footprint (2:1 iso diamond for 1×1, strip for 2×1, square for 2×2; one cell = 0.5 m), on a bare light-grey background — no terrain, no shadows. The mark is the measurement: the slicer keys the magenta, fits the mark's parallelogram, and reads off the anchor (mark centre), the ground contact patch, and the per-sprite cell size — no hand-tuned ax/ay anywhere. Verdict of this round: the convention works as designed for diamond and square footprints — six of nine props measured at 0.97–1.00 confidence with base spill under 2 px, and their art sits exactly in its claimed footprint. The three 2×1 strip footprints are the failure case: across all six generations the image model refused to paint a true 2:1 two-cell strip (it squares it, or paints it elongated but mirrored — principal axis ≈ −21° where the declared strip lies at +26.6°), so bench / planter / firewood register on a pad that does not match their declaration — the mark measures this precisely, which is the point: bad registration is now detected, not discovered in-scene. Recommendation: keep the convention; declare strip props by composing 1×1 cells or accept the measured footprint, and reserve prompt-fighting for a later round. measuring…

Part two — buildings

Buildings are the last unproven asset class before scale-up. Same registration-mark convention, three new rules proved here: (1) saturated-cyan background — the slicer keys the background by hue, globally, not by border flood, so enclosed pockets (cyan seen under a hanging sign, through a gap) cannot survive by construction; an automated check asserts zero opaque near-cyan pixels remain in every shipped sprite. (2) the door mark — a yellow diamond embedded in the magenta mark at the door's cell; the slicer detects it inside the fitted mark and records the door cell relative to the anchor: trigger placement by construction. (3) no ground — art ends at the foundation line; the engine draws contact shadows. One building per generation, style chained from the first accepted building. Verdicts: the cyan key is a clean sweep — 0 pocket pixels across all sprites, smoke and sign gaps included. Door diamonds detected in every generation (1 988–4 142 yellow px); the model moves the diamond to sit under the door it actually painted, so the measured cell tracks the art, not the declaration. Square-ish marks register at 0.74–0.86 confidence; the declared 4×3 failed twice (squared, then shattered — the blocks9 2×1 elongation refusal confirmed at building scale; evidence row below), so the bakery was re-declared 3×3, the nearest working size. The engine's y-sort handles the big footprints in all nine composites — but they also show the scale finding: at one painted cell = one engine cell (0.5 m), a 3×3 building is 1.5 m wide — dollhouse-sized against the character.
