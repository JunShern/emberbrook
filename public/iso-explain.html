The registration mark — anchors by construction

Every prop on this round's sheet was generated standing on a flat magenta registration mark shaped as its declared footprint (2:1 iso diamond for 1×1, strip for 2×1, square for 2×2; one cell = 0.5 m), on a bare light-grey background — no terrain, no shadows. The mark is the measurement: the slicer keys the magenta, fits the mark's parallelogram, and reads off the anchor (mark centre), the ground contact patch, and the per-sprite cell size — no hand-tuned ax/ay anywhere. Verdict of this round: the convention works as designed for diamond and square footprints — six of nine props measured at 0.97–1.00 confidence with base spill under 2 px, and their art sits exactly in its claimed footprint. The three 2×1 strip footprints are the failure case: across all six generations the image model refused to paint a true 2:1 two-cell strip (it squares it, or paints it elongated but mirrored — principal axis ≈ −21° where the declared strip lies at +26.6°), so bench / planter / firewood register on a pad that does not match their declaration — the mark measures this precisely, which is the point: bad registration is now detected, not discovered in-scene. Recommendation: keep the convention; declare strip props by composing 1×1 cells or accept the measured footprint, and reserve prompt-fighting for a later round. measuring…

Part two — buildings

Buildings are the last unproven asset class before scale-up. Same registration-mark convention, three new rules proved here: (1) saturated-cyan background — the slicer keys the background by hue, globally, not by border flood, so enclosed pockets (cyan seen under a hanging sign, through a gap) cannot survive by construction; an automated check asserts zero opaque near-cyan pixels remain in every shipped sprite. (2) the door mark — a yellow diamond embedded in the magenta mark at the door's cell; the slicer detects it inside the fitted mark and records the door cell relative to the anchor: trigger placement by construction. (3) no ground — art ends at the foundation line; the engine draws contact shadows. One building per generation, style chained from the first accepted building. Verdicts: the cyan key is a clean sweep — 0 pocket pixels across all sprites, smoke and sign gaps included. Door diamonds detected in every generation (1 988–4 142 yellow px); the model moves the diamond to sit under the door it actually painted, so the measured cell tracks the art, not the declaration. Square-ish marks register at 0.74–0.86 confidence; the declared 4×3 failed twice (squared, then shattered — the blocks9 2×1 elongation refusal confirmed at building scale; evidence row below), so the bakery was re-declared 3×3, the nearest working size. The engine's y-sort handles the big footprints in all nine composites — but they also show the scale finding: at one painted cell = one engine cell (0.5 m), a 3×3 building is 1.5 m wide — dollhouse-sized against the character. Buildings need either large-footprint marks (elongation risk returns) or a per-building cellScale (one painted cell = 2×2 engine cells), which the measured registration already supports. measuring…

Part three — cellScale: closing the dollhouse

cellScale is the approved fix for part two's scale finding: instead of one painted cell = one 0.5 m engine cell, each building declares a per-building cellScale S and one painted cell maps to an S×S block of engine cells — engine footprint = painted × S, render scale = S·64 / measured cellPx (so one painted cell spans exactly S engine cells on screen), the same measured anchor pixel, door cell → S×S engine door block, and a doorstep of S walkable cells outside it. Nothing about the registration convention changes — the mapping is pure arithmetic on the measured metrics (tools/bldg_cellscale.py injects it into bldg-metrics.json). Verdict of this round: at S=2 all three buildings read human-proportioned — Vesper's head sits at the lintel, not the knee — the engine sort stays correct with the scaled key (0% of the character covered in front and at the doorstep, 87–95% hidden behind the roofline), and buildings, native-1× props and the character finally sit in one coherent world. All three clear the resolution floor (painted cellPx / S ≥ 64 px per engine cell: 99.3 / 99.3 / 74.4) — nothing upscales at native zoom — but guildhall's headroom is thin (1.16×): any display zoom above that starts interpolating its stonework before any 1× prop goes soft. Standing rule from here: generate buildings so the measured painted cell is ≥ S·64 px (≥ 128 for S=2); 1024 output holds that up to 4×4 — bigger footprints need 1536+. measuring…

Part four — interiors: floor registration

Interiors are the last unproven asset class, and the shipped bakery interior is the proto's one hand-rescued asset: eyeballed 0.873 slope squash, a guessed walkable rectangle, a hand-sliced door wall — and collision that drifts from the art. This round inverts the prop convention to fix that: props stand on a magenta mark; a room wraps around one. The shell is generated with its whole walkable floor painted as one flat magenta plane reaching exactly to the wall bases — the floor mark IS the room's registration: its parallelogram fit gives the grid, the cell size, the computed slope rectification, and the wall line; walls rise from the mark's edges, so the solid map derives from the fit and cannot drift from the art. The magenta keys to transparent and the engine's f-plank ground draws the real floor, so the shipped shell is walls only — which also makes the near-wall split verifiable by construction. Variant test: the painted-floor + magenta-border variant failed structurally (the model repaints the band as a halo around the room's silhouette — evidence row below); the full-magenta floor held its plane in every generation and wins. Two model refusals measured: it would not paint an abstract yellow cell on the magenta floor (4 attempts) but happily painted a yellow doormat — outside the door, its semantic home — whose centroid maps through the measured grid to the door's wall segment; and on the props sheet it stands large props beside their mark rather than on it (detected per prop by the mark's centre coverage; such props get a base-contact anchor derived from the art + measured cellPx instead). measuring…
